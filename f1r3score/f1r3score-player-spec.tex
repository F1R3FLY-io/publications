\large Specification for a Rust tool that reads a textual score\\
and plays a run of it}

\author{F1R3FLY.io}

\date{Specification --- 26 September 2026\\[2pt]
\normalsize Revision 2. Implements \emph{Scores as Processes} (second version,
as revised in \texttt{publications} commit \texttt{5740fe9})}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This specification defines \cmd{f1r3score}, a Rust command-line player and
library for the score calculus of \emph{Scores as Processes}. The player reads a
score written in a textual surface syntax, elaborates it to a closed term of the
calculus, and executes one run. At each location it matches receipts and
messages on their subjects alone, with either decoration component possibly
left open; it evaluates the graded where-clauses on the candidates, groups them
into contention sets, selects a maximal matching with probability proportional
to the product of its weights, and keeps the \emph{record} of every
communication. Playback reads each note off its record. The outputs are the
performance (the multiset of timed notes of positive length), a replayable
trace of records, a Standard MIDI File, and optionally live MIDI.

Three properties organise the design. \emph{Faithfulness}: the engine
implements the matching, reduction and resolution rules of the note literally,
with no knowledge of voices, keys, keyboards, players or idioms; those are
library definitions in the surface syntax. \emph{Exactness}: weights are exact
rationals and chance is consumed by exact interval decoding, so a score, a
chance source and a scheduler determine a run bit for bit on every platform.
\emph{Conformance}: the reference simulator \texttt{skeinsim.py} and its fifteen
tests, T0--T14, are ported as the acceptance suite with the note's thresholds.

Revision~2 follows the revised note: wildcard decorations and the matching
relation, unlabelled reduction with records and playback, the key/touch
vocabulary, and the new section on musicians and instruments (self-re-arming
keys, the $2n$-hands piano, synchronous output, the chimeric instrument).
\S\ref{ssec:changes} lists what changed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose and scope}\label{sec:scope}

\subsection{What the tool does}

Given a file \texttt{piece.score}, the command
\begin{lstlisting}
f1r3score play piece.score --chance prng:7 --notes 64 --midi piece.mid
\end{lstlisting}
parses and elaborates the score, runs it until 64 notes of positive length have
sounded or nothing more can fire, writes the performance to standard output,
and writes a MIDI file. The same run, replayed from its trace, reproduces the
same performance exactly; and because a trace holds records, it can be played
back without re-running the score at all.

\subsection{What the tool is not}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Not an interpreter on a shard.} Following the precedent set for
  the weighted-GSLT simulator, the player is a separate, offline execution path,
  with no dependency on \texttt{f1r3node-rust}, RSpace or consensus.
\item \textbf{Not F1R3Skein.} The score calculus generalises an idea from the
  instrument; the instrument's streams are one score among many (Remark~6.3).
  A drawn machine can be imported as a clause factor, nothing more.
\item \textbf{Out of scope:} tempo as a graded dimension, dynamics and
  articulation as decorations, orchestration as grading (a fan over timbres with
  the carried timbre in the view, open thread~4), complex grading, and learning
  styles from a corpus. The renderer takes a fixed tempo as a rendering
  parameter only.
\end{itemize}

\section{The semantic contract}\label{sec:contract}

This section restates, as obligations on the implementation, what the note
defines. Anything not stated here follows the note.

\begin{requirement}[Data]\label{req:data}
A score fixes a finite pitch alphabet $\Pit$ containing the rest $\rest$, a
finite duration alphabet $\Dur$ with lengths $\len{\cdot}:\Dur\to\Qnn$ in whole
notes, containing the null duration $\nulld$ with $\len{\nulld}=0$, and a finite
set of timbres. $\Pit$ and $\Dur$ \MUST{} be disjoint, since a concrete datum
fixes the polarity of its name. Two timbres are reserved and never rendered:
the dead timbre $\tau_\bot$, used by bases and key locations, and the control
timbre $\kappa$, used by synchronous output (Def.~9.3).
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}[Terms]\label{req:terms}
A decoration component is concrete or the wildcard: $\hat\tau ::= \tau \mid
\wild$ and $\hat d ::= d \mid \wild$. Names are variables or triples
$\nm{P}{\hat\tau}{\hat d}$; processes are $0$, receipts
$\mathsf{for}(y\leftarrow x\;\mathsf{where}\;\psi)P$, joins (chord receipts,
Def.~6.5), messages $x!(Q,\hat\tau,\hat d)$, parallel composition and $\ast x$.
There is no replication, no \textsf{new}, and no recursion former. A name whose
datum is $\wild$ has no polarity.
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}[Congruence and equivalence]\label{req:cong}
Structural congruence is the least congruence closed under $\alpha$-conversion
making $(\mid,0)$ a commutative monoid and satisfying
$\ast\nm{P}{\hat\tau}{\hat d}\equiv P$. Two names are equivalent when their
quoted processes are congruent and their decorations are equal, a wildcard being
equal only to a wildcard (Def.~2.3). The implementation \MUST{} decide name
equivalence exactly, and \MUST{} \emph{not} identify $@{\ast x}$ with $x$
(Rem.~2.6): the round trip is the instance relation $x \gen @{\ast x}$, used by
matching and by nothing else.
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}[Matching and communication]\label{req:comm}
A candidate is a top-level receipt and a top-level message whose subjects
\emph{match} (Defs.~2.4 and 2.7): their locations are equivalent; their timbres meet in a
concrete timbre, $\hat\tau_1 \meet \hat\tau_2 = \tau$; and their data are both
concrete and of opposite polarity. The payload $(Q,\hat\tau_3,\hat d)$ takes no
part in matching and is carried across unchanged: firing continues with
$P\{\nm{Q}{\hat\tau_3}{\hat d}/y\}$. In particular two subjects whose timbres
are both $\wild$ never communicate, nor does a subject with no polarity
(Prop.~2.10(ii)).
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}[Records and playback]\label{req:record}
Reduction is unlabelled. Each firing yields a \emph{record}: the receipt's
subject $\nm{S}{\hat\tau_1}{d_1}$, the message's subject
$\nm{T}{\hat\tau_2}{d_2}$, and the payload $(Q,\hat\tau_3,\hat d)$ (Def.~2.8).
Its note is $\nu = (\hat\tau_1\meet\hat\tau_2,\pi,\delta)$, with $\pi$ whichever
datum is a pitch and $\delta$ whichever is a duration. The engine \MUST{} emit
records and \MUST{} compute notes only through a single pure function
$\nu$ on records, the \emph{playback} function; nothing in the engine maintains a
separate label. Every record has a note; there are no silent steps.
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}[Weights and resolution]\label{req:resolve}
A candidate at a receipt with clause $\psi$ is \emph{available} iff
$\psi\llbracket c\rrbracket>0$. Contention sets are the connected components of
the relation ``shares a receipt or a message'' over available candidates. A set
is resolved by choosing a maximal matching $\mathcal M$ with probability
proportional to $\prod_{c\in\mathcal M}\psi_c\llbracket c\rrbracket$ and firing
all of it (Def.~3.3).
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}[Time]\label{req:time}
Every top-level receipt and message carries a stamp in $\Qnn$. A communication
has onset $\max(t_r,t_m)$; its timed note is $(t,\tau,\pi,\delta)$ where
$(\tau,\pi,\delta)$ is the note of its record; it stamps what its continuation
brings to top level with $t+\len{\delta}$ (a chord, with
$t+\max_i\len{\delta_i}$). Runs are onset-ordered: a set is resolved only when
its onset, the least onset of its candidates, is minimal among all sets. The
performance is the multiset of timed notes of positive length (Def.~2.11).
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}[Engine neutrality]\label{req:neutral}
The engine \MUST{} know nothing of voices, fans, keys, servers, keyboards,
players, synchronous output, chimeras, machines or idioms. Those are definitions
in the surface syntax that elaborate to terms. (This is the property
\texttt{skeinsim.py} states in its header, and it is what makes Finding~10.3
--- the reflective voice plays the notes of the step semantics --- and
Findings~10.8 and 10.9 on instruments tests of the calculus rather than of the
code.)
\end{requirement}

\begin{remark}[What the note leaves to the implementation]
Three things are parameters of the semantics rather than parts of it: the chance
source (Def.~3.3 names it a parameter, and it may be a person), the order in
which simultaneously due contention sets are resolved, and whether a set fires
a maximal matching or a single candidate. The last is \texttt{mode='one'} in the
simulator and is retained only as a diagnostic (T3). The first two are explicit
and recorded in every trace (\S\ref{sec:chance}, \S\ref{ssec:sched}).
\end{remark}

\section{The textual format}\label{sec:syntax}

\subsection{Design position}

\begin{decision}[A dialect of the f1r3|@ng control flow sublanguage]\label{dec:dialect}
The surface syntax reuses the concrete syntax of the f1r3|@ng CFL wherever the
calculus has the same former: \texttt{for (y <- x where ...) \char`\{ P \char`\}},
\texttt{x!(...)}, \texttt{|}, \texttt{*x}, \texttt{@P}. The revised note makes
the fit closer: its general name $@P$ is exactly the rholang name \texttt{@P},
and a message that leaves its payload decoration open is exactly the rholang
send \texttt{x!(Q)}. What is new is the decoration of names, the alphabet
declarations, and a small elaboration-time macro layer. The grammar is kept
small enough to be restated as a \texttt{Theory Score} in the f1r3|@ng DDL once
the Theory syntax lands in the node; the parser crate is a replaceable front end
over a stable core AST (\S\ref{sec:arch}).
\end{decision}

Files use the extension \texttt{.score} and are UTF-8. Comments are
\texttt{//} to end of line and \texttt{/* ... */}.

\subsection{Declarations}

A score file is a sequence of imports, declarations and definitions, followed by
exactly one \texttt{play} item giving the initial configuration.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=score]
score Twinkle

pitches   { r, C3, F3, G3, C4, D4, E4, F4, G4, A4 }   // r: the rest, required
durations { q = 1/4, h = 1/2 }                        // eps = 0 is implicit
timbres   { piano = gm(0) on 1, bass = gm(32) on 2 }  // GM program, MIDI channel
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{requirement}[Alphabets]\label{req:alph}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),itemsep=1pt]
\item \texttt{pitches} lists $\Pit$ \emph{in order}; that order defines
  $\mathsf{step}(k)$ (\S\ref{ssec:atoms}). \texttt{r} \MUST{} be present and is
  unordered. A pitch written in scientific notation (\texttt{C4}, \texttt{F\#3},
  \texttt{Bb2}) gets its MIDI number implicitly (C4 = 60); any other identifier
  \MUST{} be given one, \texttt{deg7 = 67}.
\item \texttt{durations} gives each length as a non-negative rational literal
  (\texttt{1/6} for a triplet eighth, \texttt{3/8} for a dotted quarter).
  \texttt{eps = 0} is always present and \MUST{} not be redeclared.
\item Ranges \MAY{} abbreviate alphabets: \texttt{pitches \char`\{ r, scale major C4
  .. C7 \char`\}}, \texttt{pitches \char`\{ r, piano88 \char`\}} (A0 to C8). Expansion is at
  elaboration time; the alphabet is always finite.
\item Identifiers in $\Pit$, $\Dur$ and the timbre set \MUST{} be pairwise
  distinct, and \MUST{} differ from \texttt{\_}, \texttt{dead} and
  \texttt{ctl}, the surface names of $\tau_\bot$ and $\kappa$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{requirement}

\begin{center}

\noindent Compound quoted processes are parenthesised: \texttt{<@(P | Q), piano,
q>}. In binder position \texttt{\_} is a binder that is not used; in decoration
position it is the wildcard; the grammar keeps the two positions apart. In the
\texttt{play} item every top-level receipt and message is stamped $0$ unless an
\texttt{at} says otherwise (an entrance).

\subsection{Definitions: the elaboration-time macro layer}

The note builds everything from abbreviations: $\mathsf N$, $\Rec$, $\Fan$,
$\mathsf{Step}$, $\Server$, $\Voice$, $\Call$, $\Resp$, and now
$\mathsf{Key}$, $\mathsf{Installed}$, synchronous output and
$\mathsf{Chimera}$. The surface syntax needs exactly this facility and no more.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=score]
def N(L: proc, tau: timbre, p: pitch, d: dur; K: proc) =
  for (_ <- <@L, tau, p>) { K } | <@L, tau, d>!(0)      // passes @0 (Section 2.5)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{requirement}[Definitions are macros]\label{req:macros}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),itemsep=1pt]
\item Parameters are sorted: \texttt{proc}, \texttt{name}, \texttt{timbre},
  \texttt{pitch}, \texttt{dur}, \texttt{clause}, \texttt{string},
  \texttt{pitchset} (a literal subset such as \texttt{\char`\{A2, E2\char`\}} or a declared
  set), finite lists and tuples of these, and \texttt{cont} (a definition of
  arity one in a name, passed by name). Timbre
  and datum arguments may be \texttt{\_}. A definition may return a name
  (\texttt{def touch(...): name = ...}).
\item Definitions are expanded at elaboration time and \MUST{} form an acyclic
  call graph. A recursive definition is rejected with a diagnostic pointing to
  \S5.2 of the note: unbounded behaviour is obtained by reflection, not by
  recursion (Rem.~2.2).
\item Expansion is capture-avoiding. The elaborated term is a closed term of
  the core syntax and nothing else.
\end{enumerate}
\end{requirement}

\paragraph{Finite products.} $\prod_{v\in\Dur^+}\prod_{t\in\Pit}$ is written as
a comprehension that expands to a parallel composition:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=score]
def Fan(x: name, tau: timbre, over: pitchset) =
  par v in durations+, t in over {
    <@*x, tau, v>!( <@*x, tau, v>!(*x, tau, t), tau, t )
  }
\end{lstlisting}
Generators range over \texttt{pitches}, \texttt{pitches-r} (the ordered
pitches), \texttt{durations}, \texttt{durations+}, declared pitch sets, and
explicit lists.

\begin{remark}[Fans over a subset]
A voice's fan offers $|\Dur^+|\cdot|\Pit|$ messages per note. With an 88-key
alphabet, needed for a piano, that is 440 per note for five durations. A fan
over a declared pitch set $\Pit' \subseteq \Pit$ is the same score as the full
fan with every pitch outside $\Pit'$ given weight $0$ by a crisp factor; it is
offered in \texttt{std.score} as an optimisation with that meaning, and a test
\MUST{} check the equivalence.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Lines.} A written part is a right-nested chain of written notes. The
derived form
\begin{lstlisting}[language=score]
line(Lm, piano) [ C4 q, C4 q, G4 q, G4 q, A4 q, A4 q, G4 h ]
\end{lstlisting}
elaborates to $\mathsf N_{Lm,\mathsf{piano}}(\mathrm{C4},\mathsf q;\,\cdots\,
\mathsf N_{Lm,\mathsf{piano}}(\mathrm{G4},\mathsf h;\,0)\cdots)$. An optional
trailing \texttt{then K} replaces the final $0$ with a continuation, which is
how a written phrase hands off to a responder (\S7.4 of the note).

\paragraph{Synchronous output.} Definition~9.3's sequencing is surface
syntax:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=score]
touch(P1, A3, q)!(0) ; touch(P1, C3, q)!(0) ; ( touch(P1, A3, h)!(0) | touch(P1, E3, h)!(0) ) ; 0
\end{lstlisting}
A step is one touch or a parenthesised parallel group of touches; \texttt{;}
associates to the right and binds more tightly than \texttt{|}, so
\texttt{a ; b ; 0 | c ; 0} is two players in parallel. Each \texttt{;} elaborates to the payload-augmented
touches and $\mathsf{Wait}^m_A$ of Def.~9.3, with a location $A$ fresh for that
elaboration site (\S\ref{sec:open}, item~\ref{dec:ackfresh}) and the control timbre
\texttt{ctl}.

\paragraph{Locations.} A \emph{base} is written \texttt{base "Lm"} and
elaborates to the closed receipt
$\mathsf{for}(\_\leftarrow\nm{0}{\tau_\bot}{\rest})\,0$ tagged with the string;
distinct tags give inequivalent bases. A \emph{key location} is written
\texttt{keyloc(P, k)} and elaborates to
$K_{P,k} = \nm{B_P}{\tau_\bot}{k}!(0,\wild,\wild)$ (\S9.1), $B_P$ being the
keyboard's base. User code \MUST{} not write \texttt{dead}; only these two
builtins emit it.

\subsection{The standard library}

\begin{requirement}[\texttt{std.score}]\label{req:std}
The distribution \MUST{} ship \texttt{std.score}, written entirely in the
surface syntax, defining: \texttt{N}, \texttt{line}; \texttt{Rec}, \texttt{Fan},
\texttt{Seed} ($H_0$); \texttt{Step}, \texttt{Req}, \texttt{Server},
\texttt{Refresh}, \texttt{Voice} (\S5.2); \texttt{Call}, \texttt{Resp}
(handoff, including the wildcard-timbre responder of \S7.4); \texttt{Hocket}
over two players with two timbres; the dual (rhythm-leads) \texttt{Fan} and
\texttt{Step}; and the instrument layer of \S\ref{ssec:instlib}. \texttt{Voice}
\MUST{} be the reflective voice, with no engine support: its server keeps its
own code on a code channel and re-sends it (Prop.~5.3), and every request
passes the general name of the new location.
\end{requirement}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=score]
// std.score, excerpt: the reflective voice
def Req(z: name, tau: timbre, BS: proc) = <@BS, tau, eps>!(*z)     // passes @*z

def Server(tau: timbre, psi: clause, BS: proc, BC: proc) =
  for (w <- <@BS, tau, r>) {                                       // w = @*z
    Step(w, tau, psi, Req(_, tau, BS)) | Refresh(tau, BC)
  }

def Refresh(tau: timbre, BC: proc) =
  for (c <- <@BC, tau, r>) { *c | <@BC, tau, eps>!(*c) }

def Voice(tau: timbre, psi: clause, p0: pitch, u0: dur, tag: string) =
  Server(tau, psi, base tag ++ "/S", base tag ++ "/C")
  | <@(base tag ++ "/C"), tau, eps>!(Server(tau, psi, base tag ++ "/S",
                                            base tag ++ "/C"))
  | <@(base tag ++ "/S"), tau, eps>!(Seed(base tag, tau, p0, u0))
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{The instrument layer}\label{ssec:instlib}

  for (y <- <@keyloc(P, k), tau, k> where psi) { *y | KeyRefresh(P, k, tau) }

def KeyRefresh(P: proc, k: pitch, tau: timbre) =
  for (c <- <@keycode(P, k, tau), tau, r>) { *c | <@keycode(P, k, tau), tau, eps>!(*c) }

def Installed(P: proc, k: pitch, tau: timbre, psi: clause) =
  Key(P, k, tau, psi) | <@keycode(P, k, tau), tau, eps>!(Key(P, k, tau, psi))

def Keyboard(P: proc, tau: timbre, psi: clause, over: pitchset) =
  par k in over { Installed(P, k, tau, psi) }

def Chimera(P: proc, k: pitch, voices: [(timbre, clause)]) =
  par (tau, psi) in voices { Installed(P, k, tau, psi) }

def touch(P: proc, k: pitch, d: dur): name = <@keyloc(P, k), _, d>   // timbre left open

\subsection{Diagnostics and lints}

The elaborator \MUST{} reject: overlapping alphabets; undeclared data or
timbres; recursive definitions; ill-sorted arguments; \texttt{dead} in user
code; clauses outside the checkable fragment (\S\ref{ssec:fragment}). It
\SHOULD{} warn, citing Prop.~2.10(ii), when a receipt's or a message's subject
has datum \texttt{\_} (it has no polarity and can never communicate), and when a
receipt and a message at one location both leave the timbre open with nothing
else there to meet them. Revision~1's warning about a payload timbre that
differs from its subject's is withdrawn: under the revised rules that is how a
line passes between instruments.

\subsection{Terms and hash-consing}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rust]
pub struct Datum(u16);                    // index into Pi + Delta (disjoint)
pub struct Timbre(u16);                   // includes the reserved DEAD and CTL
pub enum Hat<T> { Is(T), Wild }           // a decoration component
pub enum Polarity { Channel, CoChannel }  // defined only for Hat::Is(datum)
pub struct ProcId(u32);                   // hash-consed, congruence-normal process

pub enum Name {
    Var(VarIdx),
    Quote { proc: ProcId, timbre: Hat<Timbre>, datum: Hat<Datum> },
}

pub enum Proc {                           // normal form only
    Nil,
    Par(Box<[ProcId]>),                   // flattened, >= 2, sorted by id, no Nil
    Recv { subjects: Box<[Name]>, clause: ClauseId, body: ProcId },
    Send { subj: Name, payload: ProcId, ptimbre: Hat<Timbre>, carry: Hat<Datum> },
    Drop(Name),                           // on a variable only

impl Hat<Timbre> { pub fn meet(self, other: Self) -> Option<Self>; }  // None: no match
pub fn instance_of(x: &Name, general: &Name) -> bool;                  // x below general
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{requirement}[Congruence by construction]\label{req:nf}
The arena \MUST{} intern only normal forms: parallel compositions flattened,
$0$ removed, children sorted by \texttt{ProcId} (a multiset), singletons
unwrapped; $\ast\nm{P}{\hat\tau}{\hat d}$ replaced by $P$ whatever the
decoration; bound variables in de Bruijn form, so that $\alpha$-equivalent terms
are identical. Then $P\equiv Q$ iff their ids are equal, and location
equivalence is integer equality. \texttt{Hat::Wild} is equal only to
\texttt{Hat::Wild}. A property test \MUST{} check interning against an
independent, unoptimised congruence decision procedure on random terms with
wildcards, and \MUST{} check Proposition~2.5 on them: $x \gen @{\ast x}$,
$@{\ast x}$ is greatest at its location, and $\ast@{\ast x} \equiv \ast x$.
\end{requirement}

\begin{remark}[Why sorting by id is sound]
Children are interned before their parent, so sorting by id is a canonical
order on already-normal children. Ids depend on interning order; they are never
serialised as identities. Serialised terms and digests use a structural
encoding (\S\ref{ssec:digest}).
\end{remark}

\begin{requirement}[Substitution]\label{req:subst}
Firing substitutes the name $\nm{Q}{\hat\tau_3}{\hat d}$ built from the
payload --- decoration components possibly $\wild$ --- for the bound variable
and re-normalises. Substitution \MUST{} be memoised on (body, name), since the
same body is instantiated repeatedly by a voice and by every re-armed key.
\end{requirement}

\subsection{The soup}

The running configuration is not stored as a term. As in the simulator, it is a
\emph{soup}: the top-level receipts and messages, each an \emph{occurrence} with
a stable occurrence number and a stamp, indexed by \emph{location alone}.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rust]
pub struct Loc(ProcId);                   // the quote; timbre is NOT part of the key
pub struct RecvOcc { id: OccId, subjects: Box<[Subject]>, clause: ClauseId,
                     body: ProcId, stamp: Time, label: StreamLabel }
pub struct SendOcc { id: OccId, subj: Subject, payload: ProcId,
                     ptimbre: Hat<Timbre>, carry: Hat<Datum>, stamp: Time }
pub struct Subject { loc: Loc, timbre: Hat<Timbre>, datum: Hat<Datum> }
pub struct Soup {
    recv: BTreeMap<Loc, Vec<RecvOcc>>,
    send: BTreeMap<Loc, Vec<SendOcc>>,
    dirty: BTreeSet<Loc>,
    next_occ: OccId,
}
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{requirement}[Location-only index]\label{req:locindex}
The index key \MUST{} be the location alone; each occurrence keeps its own
subject timbre, which may be $\wild$. Indexing by (location, timbre), as
revision~1 and the first simulator did, would miss every candidate in which one
side leaves its timbre open --- every touch on a keyboard, every chimera --- and
would split contention sets that the note makes one (Example~9.7).
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}[Determinism of iteration]\label{req:btree}
Every collection whose iteration order can influence a run \MUST{} be ordered
(\texttt{BTreeMap}, sorted \texttt{Vec}), keyed by occurrence number or by a
structural key. Occurrence numbers are assigned in firing order. Consequently a
score, a chance source and a scheduler determine the run exactly.
\end{requirement}

Spawning a process at stamp $t$ decomposes its normal form: \texttt{Par} spawns
each child; \texttt{Recv} and \texttt{Send} become occurrences at their
subjects' locations; \texttt{Nil} does nothing. A top-level $\ast y$ cannot occur
in a closed term.

\subsection{Digests}\label{ssec:digest}

Every elaborated score has a \emph{digest}: SHA-256 over a canonical,
id-independent serialisation of its term, alphabets and lengths. The digest
heads every trace and performance file and is the identity under which a score
would later be stored as a content-addressed value on a shard.

\subsection{Grammar}

The two sorts of Paper~II, with the atoms of Definition~3.1 of the note.

\noindent In message and receipt patterns, \texttt{?} matches any location,

patterns nest. Graded formulae have no negation (Paper~II, Definition~2.4).

\subsection{Atoms and the candidate view}\label{ssec:atoms}

A clause is evaluated on a candidate $c$ against the configuration in which it
stands. The view is
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rust]
pub struct View<'a> {
    pub timbre: Timbre,                     // the meet: always concrete
    pub pitch: Datum, pub dur: Datum,       // the note nu(record(c))
    pub carry: Hat<Datum>,                  // kappa(c); may be open
    pub ptimbre: Hat<Timbre>,               // the payload's timbre; may be open
    pub loc: ProcId,                        // @S
    pub passes: ProcId,                     // Q of <@Q, ptimbre, carry>
    pub chord: &'a [ChordSlot],             // joins only
    pub residue: Lazy<'a, Residue>,         // behavioural atoms only
}
\end{lstlisting}
\noindent The view is built from the candidate's prospective record by the
same playback function $\nu$ that renders performances (R\ref{req:record}), so
what a clause sees as the note and what is heard cannot diverge.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{9.3cm}@{}}
\toprule
atom & holds when \\
\midrule
\texttt{pitch(s)}, \texttt{dur(v)} & the note's pitch, its duration equal the
  argument \\
\texttt{carry(e)} & the carried datum is concrete and equals \texttt{e};
  \texttt{carry(\_)} holds when it is open \\
\texttt{step(k)} & carry and pitch are concrete ordered pitches and
  $\mathrm{ord}(\kappa)-\mathrm{ord}(\pi)=k$ \\

\texttt{passes(s)} & the passed process satisfies $\sigma$ \\

  chord receipt \\

\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{remark}[The note's timbre and the payload's timbre are not atoms]
Definition~3.1 exposes the note through $\atm{pitch}$ and $\atm{dur}$ only; a
clause sits on a receipt whose own timbre is usually fixed, as each key of a
chimera is. The view carries \texttt{timbre} and \texttt{ptimbre} for playback
and for the reserved orchestration extension (open thread~4), but no atom reads
them in this version. Adding \texttt{timbre(t)} and \texttt{ctimbre(t)} is the
whole of the clause-side work that open thread would need.
\end{remark}

\begin{requirement}[Derived atoms are sugar]\label{req:derived}
\texttt{held}, \texttt{prev}, \texttt{back}, \texttt{last} \MUST{} elaborate to
spatial formulae. The evaluator \MAY{} compile them to direct walks over the
record chain (as the simulator's \texttt{back} and \texttt{repeat} do), but a
test \MUST{} check the compiled and the formula forms agree on random views,
exactly as T9 does for tables.
\end{requirement}

\subsection{Tables and machines}

\texttt{table key \char`\{ k: w, ..., \_: w \char`\}} is a sum over mutually exclusive crisp
conjunctions evaluated by lookup, keyed by \texttt{dur}, \texttt{(pitch,
carry)}, \texttt{(prev, dur)}, \texttt{step}, or any tuple of atoms that are
functions of the view. \texttt{\_} is the default (absent means 0); ranges such
as \texttt{-2..2} are allowed on \texttt{step}. The \texttt{dur} key is what a
chimera's articulation clauses use (Example~9.7):
\begin{lstlisting}[language=score]
factor vibes_temper   = table dur { e: 9, q: 1, h: 1 }
factor strings_temper = table dur { e: 1, q: 1, h: 9 }
\end{lstlisting}

\texttt{machine pitch \char`\{ s -> t @ w, ... \char`\}} and \texttt{machine dur \char`\{ u -> v @ w,
...\char`\}} are Example~6.2's factors: tables keyed by \texttt{(pitch, carry)} and
\texttt{(prev, dur)}. The arrow syntax is that of the \texttt{Machines} Theory
in the drawn-distributions design; \texttt{machine pitch import "file.machine"}
reads a canonical machine exported by the instrument, mapping its state indices
onto the declared pitch alphabet by position (the rest as the last state).
\texttt{machine dur uniform} is the complete uniform machine.

\subsection{Evaluation}

\begin{requirement}[Exact weights]\label{req:exact}
Clause values are exact non-negative rationals (\texttt{BigRational}, with a
fast path for \texttt{u64/u64} that promotes on overflow). Decimal literals are
read exactly (\texttt{0.3} is $3/10$). No floating point enters a weight, a
normalisation, or a choice.
\end{requirement}

\begin{requirement}[Purity]\label{req:pure}
Evaluation is a pure function of the view and the configuration. Evaluating a
clause on a candidate that does not fire changes nothing.
\end{requirement}

$\otimes$ short-circuits on $0$; $\oplus$ sums; $\crisp\beta$ is $1$ or $0$.
Graded fixed points are evaluated only inside the checkable fragment, by
iteration to the exact fixed point over a finite state space; if the iteration
does not reach an exact fixed point within the declared bound the score is
rejected at elaboration, never approximated at run time.

\subsection{The checkable fragment}\label{ssec:fragment}

The ideal logic defines; the fragment decides. As in \texttt{rho-weighted}, the
distinction is a type, not a comment.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rust]
pub struct Checked<F> { formula: F, class: Locality, depth: usize }
pub enum Locality { Local, Behavioural { window: usize } }
impl<F> Checked<F> { pub fn try_new(f: F, alph: &Alphabets) -> Result<Self, WhyNot>; }
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{requirement}[Fragment membership]\label{req:fragment}
A clause is admitted iff it is in one of two classes.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Local}: no behavioural modality, no \texttt{leads}. Its value
  depends only on the view. Spatial formulae are decidable on the finite
  quoted terms involved; separating conjunction is decided by enumerating splits
  of a multiset and \MUST{} be bounded by a configured maximum split count.
\item \textbf{Window-behavioural}: behavioural modalities and fixed points are
  admitted when every spatial formula in \emph{every} clause of the score reads
  the past to depth at most $d$ and the alphabets are finite. Then the state
  relevant to the formula is finite (\S7.2), and $\nu$ is computed exactly by
  Knaster--Tarski iteration over the abstract graph whose nodes are
  configurations with every location truncated to depth $d$ and stamps erased.
\end{enumerate}
Anything else is rejected with \texttt{WhyNot} naming the offending subformula.
\end{requirement}

\begin{decision}[Behavioural modalities read the actual company]\label{dec:company}
In Proposition~7.2 the contexts $K$ range over ``the one-hole contexts supplied
by the rest of the score''. A player has exactly one such context at each
moment: the rest of the current configuration. The player evaluates
$\langle K\rangle$ and \texttt{leads} against that, and the \texttt{<>} surface
form carries no explicit context. This is consistent with Remark~2.9: in an
open system the context label supplies the missing half of a record, and here
the missing half is always present in the soup. Quantifying over hypothetical
companies is an analysis task, not a playing task, and is out of scope.
\end{decision}

\begin{requirement}[Locality classes drive caching]\label{req:locality}
Local clauses depend only on the candidate, so a location's candidate list is
recomputed only when that location is dirty. If any receipt in the soup carries
a behavioural clause, every such receipt's candidates are re-evaluated after
every resolution. The trace header records which regime the run used.
\end{requirement}

\section{The engine}\label{sec:engine}

\subsection{One resolution}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Candidates.} For each dirty location, pair every receipt with
  every message there whose subject matches it (R\ref{req:comm}): timbres meet
  concretely, data concrete and of opposite polarity. For a join, take every
  injective assignment of matching messages to its patterns, all at that
  location. Build the prospective record, the view from it, evaluate the
  clause, keep the candidates of positive weight.
\item \textbf{Contention sets.} Union--find over shared receipt or message
  occurrence numbers, per location. Communication requires equivalent
  locations, so a set never spans two locations (the engine \MUST{} assert
  this); it may span several timbres, as at a chimeric key touched with the
  timbre open.
\item \textbf{Onsets.} A candidate's onset is $\max(t_r,t_m)$ (for a join, the
  maximum over all its stamps); a set's onset is the least of its candidates'.
\item \textbf{Due sets.} Let $t_{\min}$ be the least onset over all sets. The
  due sets are those with onset $t_{\min}$; the scheduler picks one
  (\S\ref{ssec:sched}).
\item \textbf{Alternatives.} Enumerate the maximal matchings of the chosen set
  in canonical order, each with weight $\prod_{c\in\mathcal M}\psi\llbracket
  c\rrbracket$.
\item \textbf{Choice.} Ask the set's chance source for an index with
  probability proportional to the weights (\S\ref{sec:chance}).
\item \textbf{Firing.} For each candidate in the matching: remove both
  occurrences, emit its record with its onset, form the continuation
  $P\{\nm{Q}{\hat\tau_3}{\hat d}/y\}$ and spawn it at the new stamp. Mark
  affected locations dirty. Append one trace event.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{requirement}[Canonical order of alternatives]\label{req:canon}
Candidates are ordered by (receipt occurrence number, message occurrence
numbers); matchings by the lexicographic order of their sorted candidate lists.
Together with R\ref{req:btree}, this makes ``the $i$-th alternative'' a
well-defined, replayable quantity.
\end{requirement}

\subsection{Maximal matchings}

\begin{requirement}[Stars and the general case]\label{req:matchings}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Star fast path.} If every candidate in a set shares one receipt,
  or every candidate shares one message, the maximal matchings are the single
  candidates. This covers every voice, every chord receipt, every null note, and
  every open-timbre touch at a chimeric key (one message, several keys), and
  \MUST{} be $O(\text{set size})$ with no enumeration.
\item \textbf{General case.} Otherwise enumerate maximal matchings of the
  bipartite (for joins, hypergraph) contention structure by backtracking with
  maximality pruning. The number of alternatives \MUST{} be bounded by a
  configurable limit (default $10^5$); exceeding it is a run-time error naming
  the location, never a silent truncation. Several players reaching for one key
  at once (Example~9.5 with a third player) is a small general case.
\item \textbf{Mode \texttt{one}.} A diagnostic mode, off by default, in which
  a set fires a single candidate chosen with probability proportional to its
  weight. It exists to reproduce T3's second column and is recorded in the
  trace header.
\end{enumerate}
\end{requirement}

\subsection{Scheduling simultaneous sets}\label{ssec:sched}

When several sets are due at the same onset, the note is indifferent for
independent parts (Theorems~2.14 and 8.1) and the order matters only where
parts contend. The player makes the order an explicit, recorded parameter.

\begin{decision}[Schedulers]\label{dec:sched}
\texttt{canonical} (default): the due set whose location has the least
structural key (the digest of its quote). \texttt{random:SEED}: a dedicated
PRNG, separate from every chance source, shuffles the due sets.
\texttt{by-timbre:A,B,...}: a stated priority by the timbre of each set's
least candidate's note, for the Twinkle-style ``melody first / bass first''
tests. The scheduler never consumes digits from a musical chance source.
\end{decision}

\noindent Scheduling is audible where parts contend: in Example~9.5 with a
third player, whether the first player's A3 sounds at $0$ or waits until
$\tfrac14$, taking the rest of that phrase with it, is decided by which due set
fires first. T13 therefore runs 20 random schedules and requires exactly two
performances.

\subsection{Time}

Stamps are exact rationals. Continuations spawned by a firing receive the stamp
of R\ref{req:time}. A re-armed key is therefore available again at $t+\len\delta$
and a touch waiting for it has onset at least that (Prop.~9.2); nothing in the
engine implements ``a key sounds once at a time'' --- it follows from stamps and
consumption. Joins use the ``longest duration'' convention of Def.~6.5; since
the note lists this as open thread~5, the convention is a single function in the
engine, not a scattered assumption.

\subsection{Termination and productivity}

A run stops at the first of: no available candidate anywhere (the score has
ended or every part has fallen silent); a \texttt{--notes N} limit on notes of
positive length; a \texttt{--until T} limit on onsets (whole notes); a
\texttt{--max-steps} limit on resolutions. Installed keys that are never touched
again hold receipts forever; a finished piece on an instrument ends because no
touch remains, not because the soup is empty.

\begin{requirement}[Unproductive loops]\label{req:productive}
The note does not yet give a syntactic productivity check (open thread~6). The
engine \MUST{} bound the number of consecutive zero-length notes at one onset
(default $10^5$) and stop with an \texttt{unproductive} error naming the
locations involved. A voice plays exactly two null notes per note (Prop.~5.3)
and a synchronously written touch exactly two (Prop.~9.4), so the bound is never
approached by well-formed library code.
\end{requirement}

\subsection{Collection of inert locations}

A voice leaves $|\Dur^+|\cdot|\Pit|-1$ inert messages and $|\Pit|-1$ inert keys
behind at every note (Findings~10.1 and 10.3). Keeping them is correct but
costs memory linear in the length of the piece.

\begin{finding}[The simulator's collection test is unsound with instruments]\label{find:gc}
\texttt{skeinsim.py} collects a location whose quote is a message
(\texttt{node(loc[0])[0] == 'send'}) once it has no candidate, justified by
freshness. The revised note's key locations are messages too:
$K_{P,k} = \nm{B_P}{\tau_\bot}{k}!(0,\wild,\wild)$. Between a strike and the
refresh that re-arms it, a key location can have no candidate; collecting it
then would discard waiting touches, and the refresh would spawn the new key at a
location the collector believed dead. The simulator is safe only by accident of
representation --- it interns key locations as opaque \texttt{('key', P, k)}
nodes, not as messages --- and its T13 and T14 do not turn collection on. The
criterion cannot be carried over as written to an implementation that builds
key locations as the note defines them.
\end{finding}

\begin{requirement}[Freshness collection]\label{req:gc}
Collection is off by default. With \texttt{--gc freshness}, collection applies
only to \emph{record-shaped} locations: quotes of the form
$\nm{H}{\hat\tau}{v}!(H,\hat\tau',t)$, a message whose payload is the quote of
its own subject's location --- the shape of $\Rec$ and of nothing in the
instrument layer. After a resolution at a record-shaped location that then has
no available candidate, its occurrences are dropped and its key added to a
tombstone set. If anything is later spawned at a tombstoned location, the engine
\MUST{} stop with \texttt{freshness-violated}: the collection was unsound for
this score. For scores built from \texttt{std.score} voices this cannot happen
(Lemma~5.5), and key locations are never candidates for collection.
\end{requirement}

\begin{remark}[Why not reachability]
Collecting a location because no live term mentions it is not sound in this
calculus: any process can quote any closed term it can build, and bases and key
locations are built from nothing. Freshness is a theorem about how voices use
locations; Proposition~9.6 is the opposite fact about keys (``nothing at a key is
ever left unchosen for good''). The player relies on each only where it holds.
\end{remark}

\section{Chance sources}\label{sec:chance}

\subsection{One interface}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rust]
pub trait Chance {
    /// Choose an index into `weights` (exact, all > 0) with probability
    /// proportional to weight. Returns the index and what was consumed.
    fn choose(&mut self, weights: &[Weight], ctx: &ChoiceCtx) -> (usize, Consumed);
    fn describe(&self) -> SourceSpec;          // recorded in the trace header
}
pub trait DigitStream { fn base(&self) -> u32; fn next(&mut self) -> u32; }
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{decision}[Every automatic source is a digit stream]\label{dec:digits}
Every non-human source is a stream of digits in some base, and every choice is
made by the exact interval decoding of the drawn-distributions design
(Algorithm~1): cumulative weights are cleared to a common denominator, digits
narrow a $b$-adic interval, and the choice is the bin that contains it. There is
no floating point and no rejection sampling, so a given digit stream yields the
same choices on every machine. $K_{\max}$ (default 64 digits per choice) and its
deterministic fallback are part of the source's specification.
\end{decision}

\subsection{Which source a choice uses}

Theorem~8.1(iii) holds \emph{surely} only when each voice has its own source
(T2 uses one stream per voice). The player routes choices through \emph{stream
labels} (revision~1 called them stream keys; the note now uses ``key'' for a
receipt holding a pitch).

\begin{decision}[Stream labels]\label{dec:streams}
Every receipt occurrence carries a stream label, inherited from the receipt that
spawned it. By default the label is the receipt's subject timbre when that is
concrete, and otherwise the digest of its location. It \MAY{} be set explicitly
with an annotation \texttt{for \#A (...)} or on a definition application
\texttt{Voice(...) \#A}; annotations are metadata and change neither the
elaborated term's congruence class nor its digest. The command line maps labels
to sources: \texttt{--chance prng:7 --stream A=spigot:pi/16 --stream B=prng:12}.
A contention set uses the stream of its least receipt occurrence; at a chimeric
key that is the first-installed timbre's stream, which is why a chimera is
normally given one explicit label (\texttt{Chimera(...) \#K}). Sets with a
single alternative (every null note) consume nothing.
\end{decision}

\subsection{Factorised resolution}

Lemma~6.4 lets a star whose weights factor as $f(a)g(b)$ be resolved by two
independent draws. T4 depends on this: pitch from the hexadecimal digits of
$\pi$, duration from the base-5 digits of $e$, one digit each.

\begin{requirement}[Factor routing]\label{req:factor}
When a star's clause is a product of factors that are functions of disjoint
parts of the view (the elaborator can see this syntactically: a
\texttt{(pitch, carry)} machine times a \texttt{(prev, dur)} machine), the
engine \MAY{} resolve it factor by factor, each factor drawing from its own
stream (\texttt{--stream A.pitch=spigot:pi/16 --stream A.dur=spigot:e/5}). This
is an equivalent resolution by Lemma~6.4 and \MUST{} be recorded in the trace.
Without a per-factor stream the joint draw is used.
\end{requirement}

\subsection{Human choice}\label{ssec:human}

Proposition~7.4 makes the improviser a chance source that ignores the
probabilities and chooses among available candidates. Section~9 locates that
choice: in the instrument division a player only sends, and choice is made at
receipts, so an improvising player needs receipts of its own --- listening to a
lead sheet, to the band, or to a fan of phrases it offers itself. A human bound
to a stream label is choosing at exactly those receipts.

\begin{requirement}[Human chooser]\label{req:human}
The \texttt{human} source presents, for each set with more than one
alternative, the alternatives as notes (timbre, pitch, duration, carried datum),
with their normalised probabilities shown for guidance, and accepts an index.
Only alternatives of positive weight are offered, so every trace a human
produces is admissible, and with a viability factor never runs out
(Prop.~7.4(iii)). Mixed performance --- a human on one stream label, algorithmic
sources on the others --- is the default when \texttt{human} is bound to a
single label. In this version the human chooser is step-wise, not real time.
\end{requirement}

\section{Outputs}\label{sec:outputs}

\subsection{Playback}

\begin{requirement}[Playback is a function on records]\label{req:playback}
\texttt{score-render} \MUST{} expose $\nu:\mathrm{Record}\to\mathrm{Note}$ and
compute every note it prints, writes or sends by applying it to a record. The
engine calls the same function to build views. A playback of a run is the
sequence of notes of its records; the performance is the multiset of its timed
notes of positive length. Given a trace, playback needs neither the score nor
the engine (\S\ref{sec:cli}, \texttt{render}).
\end{requirement}

\subsection{Performance}

The performance is printed as a table by default and as JSON with
\texttt{--out perf.json}:
\begin{lstlisting}
{ "score": "sha256:...", "end": "1",
  "notes": [ { "onset": "0", "timbre": "piano", "pitch": "A3", "dur": "q", "len": "1/4" },
             { "onset": "0", "timbre": "piano", "pitch": "A2", "dur": "h", "len": "1/2" }, ... ] }
\end{lstlisting}
Notes are sorted by (onset, timbre, pitch, duration) so that two runs with the
same performance produce byte-identical files; this is how interleaving
invariance is tested. Null notes are excluded; \texttt{--include-null} adds them
for diagnosis.

\subsection{Trace}

\begin{requirement}[Replayable trace of records]\label{req:trace}
With \texttt{--trace run.jsonl} the player writes JSON Lines: a header (format
version, player version, score digest, alphabets with lengths and MIDI numbers,
timbre declarations, every source spec, stream-label bindings, scheduler,
matching mode, locality regime, collection mode), then one event per
resolution: step number, onset, location digest, set size, number of
alternatives, chosen index, digits consumed per stream, and for each fired
candidate its record --- both subjects' decorations and the payload decoration,
with the payload process by digest. \texttt{f1r3score replay} \MUST{} reproduce
the event sequence from the score and the trace, and \MUST{} fail if the score's
digest differs from the header's.
\end{requirement}

\subsection{MIDI}

\begin{requirement}[Standard MIDI File]\label{req:midi}
\texttt{--midi out.mid} writes a format-1 file with one track per rendered
timbre, a program change from the timbre's declaration, and note-on/note-off
pairs at $\mathrm{ticks}(t)$ and $\mathrm{ticks}(t+\len\delta)$. A note's track
is chosen by its timbre --- the meet --- so a chimeric key's notes are routed per
note to whichever timbre won. Rests, and every note in \texttt{dead} or
\texttt{ctl}, produce no events. The division (ticks per quarter) is the least
value making every onset and length an integer number of ticks, if that is at
most 32767; otherwise 960 with rounding and a warning. Tempo is \texttt{--bpm}
(default 120), a rendering parameter only.
\end{requirement}

\begin{finding}[\texttt{spigot\_midi} cannot be reused as is]
\texttt{spigot\_midi::MidiTrack} writes format 0 from a sequence of
(pitch, duration, velocity) notes with no onsets: it is monophonic and
sequential by construction. A score is polyphonic, with explicit onsets,
simultaneous strikes and, now, per-note timbre. The player needs its own small
writer. It \SHOULD{} follow \texttt{spigot\_midi}'s dependency-free style, reuse
its \texttt{GeneralMidi} enumeration for program names, and \MAY{} later be
upstreamed to replace it.
\end{finding}

\subsection{Live MIDI}

With feature \texttt{live} and \texttt{--live [PORT]}, a playback thread
receives records from the engine through a bounded channel, applies $\nu$, and
sends notes to a MIDI port via \texttt{midir}, as \texttt{leap\_spigot::player}
does. The engine runs ahead of the clock by a configurable look-ahead in whole
notes and never waits on the clock: live output is a rendering of the same run,
and its trace is identical to an offline run's.

\section{Command line}\label{sec:cli}

\begin{lstlisting}
f1r3score check   piece.score              # parse, elaborate, lint, report fragment and digest
f1r3score expand  piece.score              # print the elaborated core term
f1r3score play    piece.score [options]    # run once
f1r3score replay  piece.score run.jsonl    # re-run from the trace's choices and compare
f1r3score render  run.jsonl [--midi out.mid] [--out perf.json]   # playback only
f1r3score machine import skein.machine --pitches piece.score     # print as a factor

options for play:
  --chance SPEC            default source (prng:SEED | spigot:C/B | human)
  --stream LABEL=SPEC      bind a stream label (repeatable; LABEL.pitch / LABEL.dur for factors)
  --scheduler S            canonical | random:SEED | by-timbre:A,B,...
  --notes N | --until T | --max-steps N
  --mode max|one           matching mode (default max)
  --gc off|freshness       collection (default off)
  --max-alternatives N     bound on maximal matchings per set (default 100000)
  --out FILE  --trace FILE  --midi FILE  --bpm N  --live [PORT]  --include-null
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent \texttt{render} is new in revision~2 and exists because reduction is
unlabelled: the trace's records already contain everything playback needs
(Def.~2.8). Exit codes: 0 success; 1 usage; 2 parse or elaboration error; 3
run-time error (\texttt{unproductive}, \texttt{freshness-violated}, alternative
limit); 4 replay mismatch.

\subsection{Additional tests}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Congruence and round trip.} Normal-form interning agrees with an
  independent decision procedure on $10^4$ random term pairs with wildcards;
  Proposition~2.5 holds on all of them; $@{\ast x}$ is never identified with
  $x$ (Rem.~2.6).
\item \textbf{Matching.} Unit tests for each clause of Defs.~2.4 and 2.7 and
  Prop.~2.10(ii): open timbre against concrete (matches, note in the concrete
  timbre); both open (never); open datum on either subject (never); equal
  polarities (never); payload decoration irrelevant to matching but carried
  into the bound name.
\item \textbf{Records.} For every conformance run, the notes the engine
  reported equal $\nu$ applied to the trace's records, and \texttt{render} on the
  trace produces the same performance and MIDI file as \texttt{play}.
\item \textbf{Cross-implementation differential.} For T0--T14 and random small
  scores, the Rust player and \texttt{skeinsim.py}, driven by the same recorded
  choice indices, emit the same records. The Python side gains a small adapter
  that accepts a choice list; this is the faithfulness obligation, kept as a
  permanent CI job.
\item \textbf{Replay.} Every conformance run's trace replays to an identical
  event sequence.
\item \textbf{Instruments.} Proposition~9.2: a key location never holds two
  receipts of one timbre; two touches aimed at one key queue. Proposition~9.4:
  a synchronous step releases its continuation at $\max_i(t_i+\len{\delta_i})$
  with two null notes per touch. Proposition~9.6: a chimera's key location holds
  exactly one receipt per timbre whenever nothing there is sounding. A hocket
  between two timbres alternates note by note (\S7.4).
\item \textbf{Enforcement.} A window-behavioural score using
  $\mathsf{Viable}_\beta$ (Prop.~7.2) runs $10^4$ notes without violating
  $\beta$ and without falling silent; the same score without the viability
  factor is shown, on a fixture, to fall silent.
\item \textbf{Chords.} A chord receipt emits $k$ notes at one onset, and its
  continuation is stamped at the longest duration.
\item \textbf{Collection.} A deliberately non-fresh score run with
  \texttt{--gc freshness} fails with \texttt{freshness-violated}; FourHands run
  with \texttt{--gc freshness} collects nothing and plays as without it
  (Finding~\ref{find:gc}).
\item \textbf{Fans over a subset} equal full fans with a crisp exclusion factor,
  record for record, under the same stream.
\item \textbf{MIDI.} Twinkle's and FourHands' files are byte-identical to golden
  files; a chimera run routes each note to its winning timbre's track.
\end{itemize}

\section{Decisions to confirm}\label{sec:open}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Home of the code.} Candidates: a new repository
  \texttt{F1R3FLY-io/f1r3score}; a directory beside the note in
  \texttt{publications/f1r3score}, where revision~1 of this specification now
  sits; or \texttt{F1R3Games}. Since the calculus is deliberately not F1R3Skein,
  the proposal is a dedicated repository, with the note's directory linking to
  it.
\item \textbf{Surface syntax.} Confirm Decision~\ref{dec:dialect}: a CFL
  dialect now, restated as \texttt{Theory Score} in the DDL later; the name
  syntax \texttt{<@P, tau, d>} with \texttt{\_} for an open component;
  \texttt{@P} and \texttt{x!(Q)} as the general-name shorthands; the file
  extension \texttt{.score}.
\item \textbf{Touch notation.} The note writes $\langle @P,k,d\rangle$ for a
  touch at key $k$ of keyboard $P$, which reuses the timbre slot for a key. The
  proposal is \texttt{touch(P, k, d)} in the surface syntax, to keep the
  triple's slots meaning one thing each.
\item \textbf{Freshness of acknowledgement locations.}\label{dec:ackfresh}
  Definition~9.3 asks for ``a fresh location $A$''. A finite term can only make
  $A$ fresh per elaboration site. That suffices for written phrases, which run
  once, but a \texttt{;} inside code that is re-run by reflection --- a player
  that is itself a self-refreshing server --- would reuse $A$ across runs, and
  acknowledgements from one run could release another. The proposal: fresh per
  site, with an elaboration error when a \texttt{;} occurs under a quote that
  can be re-run; a per-run fresh $A$ would need the record-of-the-past trick
  voices use (quote the current location into $A$), which the note may prefer
  to specify.
\item \textbf{Exact rationals by default.} Decision~\ref{dec:digits} and
  R\ref{req:exact} put bit-for-bit reproducibility ahead of speed. The proposal
  is not to add an \texttt{f64} mode until a measured need appears.
\item \textbf{Stream labels.} Decision~\ref{dec:streams} defaults to the
  receipt's timbre, or its location when the timbre is open, with optional
  labels. Confirm, and confirm that a chimera's contention set should draw from
  one stream rather than, say, the stream of the player whose touch it is.
\item \textbf{Scheduler default.} \texttt{canonical} makes runs reproducible
  without a seed. Confirm, or prefer a seeded random default. It matters
  audibly only where players contend for a key.
\item \textbf{Behavioural modalities.} Decision~\ref{dec:company} evaluates them
  against the actual company, and R\ref{req:fragment} admits them only in the
  window fragment. Confirm that a bounded-lookahead approximation, which would
  not guarantee liveness, should stay out.
\item \textbf{Timbre atoms.} No atom reads the note's timbre or the payload's in
  this version, matching Def.~3.1. Confirm, or add \texttt{timbre(t)} now as the
  first step of open thread~4.
\item \textbf{Chord continuation.} The longest-duration convention of Def.~6.5
  is isolated in one function; confirm it as the default while open thread~5 is
  pursued.
\item \textbf{Handoff record.} Proposition~7.5 notes that the outermost record
  of a handoff carries a pitch that was never played. The proposal: expose it in
  \texttt{last[...]}, and give \texttt{std.score} a \texttt{call[...]} atom that
  skips it.
\item \textbf{Pitch naming.} Scientific pitch names with C4 = 60, explicit MIDI
  numbers otherwise, enharmonic spellings distinct data (\texttt{F\#4} and
  \texttt{Gb4} are different pitches with one MIDI number).
\end{enumerate}
