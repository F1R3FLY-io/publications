\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A mortal scientist is a cost-accounted computation that learns about the
computations around it by perceiving them, interacting with them, and
reflecting on the code of those it holds. Its evidence is asymmetric: an
existential claim about an environment is confirmed by one successful
experiment, while a universal claim is only ever refuted. This note asks
what that asymmetry costs, and recasts the separation of $\Pclass$ from
$\NPclass$, if there is one, as a statement about it. Fix a bounded
specimen and ask the scientist to certify that the specimen cannot reach a
designated state. We define an \emph{experimental regime} --- a grade of
access, an intervention language, a readout and a budget --- and show that
the regimes split. Regimes restricted to interaction provably cannot
certify such universal negatives at polynomial cost: an adversary argument
forces $2^d$ intervened copies on specimens of size $O(d \log d)$. Regimes
with reflection can do so if and only if $\Pclass = \NPclass$, under three
stated cost hypotheses. We then take stock of two tools. Fuzzy rho, whose
graded communication modulates the code being sent, supplies
\emph{knockout} experiments: by choosing the channel on which a specimen
is sent, the experimenter chooses a grade that deletes pieces of it, and
with stamps, a unit-grade control and pooled readout this gives isolated,
attributable interventions. Deleting whole prefixes is a sound
intervention and keeps the interaction bound; weakening a
synchronisation defeats it. The rho
combinators supply a literal combinatorial account of where the complexity
lives: we show that the universal negative is $\coNP$-complete already for
the two atoms $\{\cm, \csy\}$, where every run is of linear length, and in
$\mathbf{NL}$ for the join-free atoms $\{\cfw, \ckk, \cdd, \cq\}$, so that
duplication alone adds no hardness. Hardness lives in synchronisation;
the cost of an experimenter lives in contraction. We combine the tools ---
regimes as points of a product lattice, knockouts on the combinator image,
the cost of sharing after Asperti, and a light namespace logic in which
the cost hypothesis becomes a type --- and close by describing what it
would mean for a mortal scientist to live in a world where
$\Pclass = \NPclass$, and in one where it does not.
\end{abstract}

% =====================================================================
\section{Aim}\label{sec:intro}
% =====================================================================

The aim of this note is to frame the $\Pclass$ versus $\NPclass$ question
as a question about experimental regimes, in the sense of the mortal
scientist \cite{Scientist}, and to state the problem in that frame in a
way a reader can follow without first reading the companion notes.

The mortal scientist is a model of learning in which a learner is a
cost-accounted computation in the reflective higher-order (rho) calculus,
its environment is other computations, its hypotheses are formulae of a
namespace logic, and its experiments are assays whose results are paid
for out of a finite token stack. Two features of that model make it a
natural setting for the question.

\paragraph{The scientist's evidence is already asymmetric.} In the
scientist's logic, an existential claim about an environment --- some
interaction leads somewhere --- is confirmed by a finite witness, while a
universal claim --- no interaction leads there --- can be refuted by a
single position but never finitely confirmed by running
\cite[Prop.~Asymmetry]{Scientist}. This is the $\NPclass$/$\coNP$
asymmetry seen from inside a learner. The scientist is never asked to
decide a language. It is asked to pay for evidence, and the question that
matters to it is what evidence for a universal negative costs.

\paragraph{The scientist's access is already graded.} A scientist has
exactly three grades of access to its environment: \emph{perception} of
the surface, \emph{interaction} by probing, and \emph{reflection} on the
code of names it holds \cite[\S2]{Scientist}. These grades are priced
differently, and they are ordered by invasiveness. The separation of
$\Pclass$ from $\NPclass$ turns out to be a statement about exactly one
of them.

\medskip
\noindent
The reframing does three things the standard formulation does not.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item It makes the \emph{apparatus} explicit. What an experimenter can do
with a specimen --- run it once, run it repeatedly, intervene on it, read
it --- becomes a choice of regime, and the known barriers to proving
$\Pclass \neq \NPclass$ appear as features of the apparatus rather than
as facts about proofs.
\item It makes the \emph{cost model} explicit. The note's central
equivalence holds only because the cost model charges for unshared work,
and Asperti's observation that $\Pclass = \NPclass$ ``up to sharing''
\cite{AspertiSharing} identifies exactly the clause that must not be
relaxed.
\item It gives two existing tools traction. Fuzzy rho \cite{FuzzyRho}
supplies controlled experiments in which pieces of the code being sent
are knocked out, the experimenter choosing what to delete by choosing
where to send. The rho combinators \cite{RhoComb} supply a
combinatorial account in which one can include or exclude individual
atoms and watch the complexity of the scientist's problem change.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Plan.} Section~\ref{sec:apparatus} assembles the apparatus:
the rho calculus, context-labelled bisimilarity, the mortal scientist,
and the cost model. Section~\ref{sec:problem} states the problem in the
frame and proves the two results that anchor it.
Sections~\ref{sec:fuzzy} and~\ref{sec:comb} take stock of fuzzy rho and
the rho combinators. Section~\ref{sec:combine} develops ways of combining
them. Section~\ref{sec:worlds} concludes with what each answer to the
question would mean for a mortal scientist. Appendix~\ref{app:status}
records the status of each result. Nothing here claims progress on
$\Pclass$ versus $\NPclass$ itself; the claim is that this is the right
laboratory in which to look at it.

\section{The apparatus}\label{sec:apparatus}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{The rho calculus}\label{sec:rho}

\begin{definition}[Syntax]
Processes $P$ and names $x$ are given by mutual recursion:
\[
P \ ::=\ \nil \ \mid\ P_1 \mid P_2 \ \mid\ \out{x}{P}
   \ \mid\ \mathsf{for}(y_1 \leftarrow x_1 \,\&\, \cdots \,\&\, y_n \leftarrow x_n)P
   \ \mid\ \drp{x}
\qquad\qquad
x \ ::=\ \quo{P}
\]
$\out{x}{P}$ is the \emph{lift}: it quotes $P$ and outputs the name
$\quo{P}$ on $x$. The input binds $y_1,\dots,y_n$ in $P$; for $n = 1$ we
write $\inp{y}{x}{P}$, and for $n > 1$ it is a \emph{join}. $\drp{x}$ is
the \emph{drop}. We write $\wild$ for a bound name that does not occur. A
process is \emph{closed} if no variable bound by an input occurs free in
it.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Name equivalence, structural congruence, reduction]
As in \cite{NWNS}: $\nequiv$ is the least equivalence on names with
$\quo{P_1} \nequiv \quo{P_2}$ whenever $P_1 \equiv P_2$, and
$\quo{(\drp{x_1})} \nequiv x_2$ whenever $x_1 \nequiv x_2$; $\equiv$ is
the least congruence containing $\alpha$-equivalence and the abelian
monoid laws for $\mid$ with unit $\nil$, names being compared up to
$\nequiv$. Reduction is
\[
\frac{x_i \nequiv x_i' \quad (1 \le i \le n)}
     {\mathsf{for}(y_1 \leftarrow x_1 \,\&\, \cdots \,\&\, y_n \leftarrow x_n)P
      \ \mid\ \out{x_1'}{Q_1} \mid \cdots \mid \out{x_n'}{Q_n}
      \ \to\ P\{\quo{Q_1}/y_1, \dots, \quo{Q_n}/y_n\}}
\]
closed under $\mid$ and $\equiv$. Substitution replaces all names
$\nequiv$-equal to the variable, reaches the object of a lift, does not
reach beneath a quote, and performs the drop:
$(\drp{x})\{\quo{P}/y\} = P$ when $x \nequiv y$.
\end{definition}

This is the calculus of \cite{RHO} with joins, in the notation of
\cite{NWNS}. A name is in \emph{normal form} if no quote in it
immediately encloses a drop; rewriting $\quo{(\drp{x})}$ to $x$
throughout gives a normal form $\nequiv$-equal to any name. Name
equivalence is decidable in polynomial time, being equality of unordered
trees after normalisation.

Three facts about names are used throughout.

\begin{proposition}[Well-foundedness]\label{prop:wf}
No process $P$ has $\quo{P}$ among its names, up to the identity
$\quo{(\drp{x})} \nequiv x$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
A name occurring in $P$ quotes a process strictly smaller than $P$, so its
quote depth is at most that of $P$, while $\quo{P}$ has quote depth one
greater. The only exception is the degenerate $P = \drp{x}$, where
$\quo{P} \nequiv x$; quote depth in the sense of \cite{Lybech22}
assigns $\quo{(\drp{x})}$ the depth of $x$ and defines it away.
\end{proof}

Consequently a name fresh for $P$ can be manufactured by quoting any term
that properly contains $P$, without a name server \cite{Scientist,NWNS},
and \texttt{new} is syntactic sugar. Self-reference is never a matter of
a term containing its own name; it can only arise at run time, by
communication.

\begin{proposition}[One quote level per rendezvous]\label{prop:onelevel}
A reduction adds at most one level of quote depth. Without joins it adds
at most one name \cite{NWNS}; an $n$-ary join adds at most $n$.
\end{proposition}

The \emph{duplicator} $\Dup(x) \triangleq \inp{y}{x}{(\drp{y} \mid
\out{x}{\drp{y}})}$ satisfies $\out{x}{Q} \mid \Dup(x) \to Q \mid
\out{x}{Q}$. Since $\quo{(\drp{y})} \nequiv y$, the duplicator re-sends
what it receives \emph{without} raising its quote depth. It is the source
of contraction on names, and of replication: $\out{x}{\Dup(x)} \mid
\Dup(x)$ runs forever.

\subsection{Context-labelled transitions}\label{sec:clb}

We do not use barbs. Following \cite{NWNS}, observation is by contexts.

\begin{definition}[Context-labelled transitions and bisimilarity]
\label{def:clbisim}
Write $P \Lab{C} P'$ when $C[P] \to P'$, and $P \WLab{C} P'$ when $C[P]
\to^* P'$. For an \emph{observer class} $\Obs \subseteq \Ctx$, a symmetric
relation $\mathcal{R}$ is a $\Obs$-bisimulation if $P \mathrel{\mathcal{R}}
Q$ and $P \Lab{C} P'$ with $C \in \Obs$ imply $Q \WLab{C} Q'$ with $P'
\mathrel{\mathcal{R}} Q'$; $\bisim_\Obs$ is the largest such. An
$\Obs$-trace of $P$ is a sequence $C_1 \cdots C_k$ of contexts in $\Obs$
with $P \WLab{C_1} P_1 \cdots \WLab{C_k} P_k$; $P \treq^\Obs Q$ when they
have the same $\Obs$-traces. Plainly $\bisim_\Obs \subseteq
\treq^\Obs$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Success]\label{def:success}
Fix a name $\ok$ and let $C_\ok = \hole \mid \inp{\wild}{\ok}{\nil}$.
$P$ \emph{can succeed} if $P \WLab{\hole} P' \Lab{C_\ok} P''$ for some
$P', P''$, the displayed step consuming an output on $\ok$.
\end{definition}

Success is a context-labelled transition, not a barb.

\begin{remark}[Holes under a lift]\label{rem:bihol}
Context-labelled bisimilarity in rho is a congruence for the operators
when observations are suitably restricted, and fails to be one for
contexts whose hole lies under a lift or a quote, because communication
depends on name equality rather than on bisimilarity \cite{BIHOL}. The contexts that
matter in \S\ref{sec:internal} are exactly of that kind.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The mortal scientist}\label{sec:mortal}

We recall from \cite{Scientist} only what is used. The premise is that
computations are ontologically isolated, so the environment of a
computation is other computations, and a world factors across an
interaction cut as $W \equiv S \mid E$, a scientist $S$ and its
environment $E$.

\paragraph{Three grades of access.} $S$ does not hold $E$'s term. It has
exactly three kinds of access, and the calculus offers no fourth
\cite[\S2.2]{Scientist}.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lllp{0.27\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
grade & instrument & applies to & price \\
\midrule
perception & structural predicates & the surface of $E$ &
monotone in formula depth \\
interaction & $\langle K_j \rangle \varphi$ & the behaviour of $E$ &
one rendezvous per step, and time \\
reflection & name predicates $@\varphi$ & names $S$ holds &
a computation on a name; no communication \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The modality $\langle K_j\rangle$ is generated by the rewrite rule and is
labelled by the redex context: for $W \equiv K_j[\inp{x}{n}{P} \mid
\out{n}{Q}]$ the label $K_j$ is everything else, so $\exists j.\,\langle
K_j\rangle\varphi$ and $\forall j.\,[K_j]\varphi$ quantify over redex
positions. The name predicate satisfies $n \models @\varphi$ iff $n =
\quo{P}$ with $P \models \varphi$; it is namespace logic \cite{NamespaceLogic}.
Reflection is cheap for the reason predation is not: opening a name one
holds is not a passage through a surface.

\paragraph{Assays.} A hypothesis installed as the guard of an input is an
experiment. The rho variant whose for-comprehension carries a
\texttt{where} clause \cite{Omnibus} realises an \emph{assay} on probe
channel $p$ as a complementary pair of guarded receipts,
\[
K[\,\cdots\,] \ \mid\ \inp{x}{p \texttt{ where } \varphi(x)}{\out{\mathit{confirm}}{\drp{x}}}
\ \mid\ \inp{x}{p \texttt{ where } \lnot\varphi(x)}{\out{\mathit{refute}}{\drp{x}}},
\]
and, when $\varphi$ is decidable on the received term within the budget,
yields one of three outcomes: \textsc{confirm}, \textsc{refute}, or $\bot$
when nothing arrives within the budget \cite[Prop.~Trichotomy]{Scientist}. A guard may test a name predicate on a
received quoted process. So in the \texttt{where}-clause variant of rho,
reflection is available operationally: a scientist that receives
$\quo{E}$ can inspect $E$'s code without running it.

\paragraph{The asymmetry.} For $\varphi = \exists j.\,\langle K_j\rangle
\psi$, \textsc{confirm} is a finite witness, while refuting it requires
ruling out every position; for $\forall j.\,[K_j]\psi$ the polarity
reverses \cite[Prop.~Asymmetry]{Scientist}. A scientist with a finite
budget can therefore test only a fragment bounded in modal depth and
fixed-point unfolding, and \emph{the budget is the inductive bias}.

\paragraph{The game.} Tokens are conserved and never minted. A mortal
computation exposes its stack as a message on a \emph{metabolic channel};
a harvest is an ordinary receipt on that channel, and it ends the prey's
computational life \cite[\S7]{Scientist}. Under \emph{capability gating}
a predator must derive a metabolic name from structure it can perceive,
and derivation is real work. Two consequences matter here.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{One specimen, one measurement.} After a destructive assay no
further assay may be run against the specimen
\cite[Prop.~One specimen]{Scientist}. This is the linear tier of
\S\ref{sec:tiers}.
\item \emph{Concealment is a price, not a wall.} Because names are quoted
processes and \texttt{new} is sugar, nothing is secret; it is only
expensive to find \cite[Rem.~Restriction is sugar]{Scientist}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cost}\label{sec:cost}

Costs are read in cost-accounted rho, whose phlogiston charges
communication \cite{CostMonad}. We need three properties of the charge,
stated as hypotheses because they are properties of a cost model, not of
the calculus.

\begin{hypothesis}[Simulation]\label{hyp:sim}
There is a polynomial $q$ such that, for every deterministic Turing
machine $M$, there is a rho process $R_M(x)$ which, given an input word
$w$ as data on $x$, runs determinately to a representation of $M(w)$ at
phlogiston at most $q(t_M(w) + |w|)$, where $t_M(w)$ is the running time.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{hypothesis}[Accounting]\label{hyp:acct}
Each communication --- including the graded communications of
\S\ref{sec:fuzzy} --- is charged at least $1$ plus the total size,
\emph{unshared}, of the transmitted terms; and every run of phlogiston $c$
from a term of size $n$, including its guard evaluations and the copying
performed by substitution into drops, can be simulated by a deterministic
Turing machine in time polynomial in $c + n$.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{hypothesis}[Reflection]\label{hyp:refl}
In the reflection tier, guards are \emph{structural}: boolean
combinations of term-former connectives and name predicates, without
behavioural modalities or fixed points. Evaluating such a guard of size
$s$ on a held name quoting a term of size $n$ costs phlogiston at least
$1$ and at most polynomial in $s + n$.
\end{hypothesis}

The second clause of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:acct} is stated as an
assumption, not derived, because it must cover steps that communication
charges alone do not meter. Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:refl} excludes guards
such as $\Diamond\langle C_\ok\rangle\top$, which the \texttt{where}
clause of \cite{Omnibus} would otherwise admit and which would decide the
problem of \S\ref{sec:problem} in one unpriced step.

The word \emph{unshared} in Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:acct} carries the whole
weight of \S\ref{sec:sharing}. Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:refl} is a constraint
on the pricing schedule of reflection; the sensing schedules used in the
companion game note \cite{GameNote}, exponential in depth, apply to
perception across the cut, not to reading a name one holds.

\section{The problem in the frame}\label{sec:problem}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Bounded specimens and the universal negative}\label{sec:absence}

The scientist's question is about a specimen whose behaviour is bounded,
so that every run is short and the only difficulty is in the branching.

\begin{definition}[Flat specimens]\label{def:flat}
A process is \emph{flat} if it is closed, contains no drop (not even
beneath a quote), and no bound name occurs in subject position. In a flat
process each communication consumes an input prefix not under a lift and
creates none, since no transmitted term is ever run; so every run of a
flat $P$ has length at most $|P|$. A \emph{race} in $P$ is a set of input
prefixes competing for one output; a \emph{resolution} of $P$ assigns a
winner to each race.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The universal negative]
$\ABS$ is the problem: given a flat $P$, decide that $P$ \emph{cannot}
succeed. In the scientist's logic this is the universal formula
$\lnot\,\exists\,\text{run}.\,\Diamond\langle C_\ok\rangle\top$ --- a
claim that no position, at any point of any run, offers $\ok$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:abscomplete}
$\ABS$ is $\coNP$-complete.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
It is in $\coNP$: a successful run has length at most $|P|$ and is a
certificate of success, checkable in polynomial time because name
equivalence is. For hardness, reduce from CNF unsatisfiability. Given
variables $v_1, \dots, v_d$ and clauses $c_1, \dots, c_m$, fix pairwise
distinct closed names $b_i, t_{ij}, f_{ij}, e_j, \ok$ and let
\[
\mathit{Race}_i \;=\; \out{b_i}{\nil}
  \ \mid\ \inp{\wild}{b_i}{\textstyle\prod_{j : v_i \in c_j} \out{t_{ij}}{\nil}}
  \ \mid\ \inp{\wild}{b_i}{\textstyle\prod_{j : \neg v_i \in c_j} \out{f_{ij}}{\nil}},
\]
let $\mathit{Clause}_j$ compose $\inp{\wild}{t_{ij}}{\out{e_j}{\nil}}$
for each positive and $\inp{\wild}{f_{ij}}{\out{e_j}{\nil}}$ for each
negative occurrence in $c_j$, and put
$P_\varphi = \prod_i \mathit{Race}_i \mid \prod_j \mathit{Clause}_j \mid
\mathsf{for}(\wild \leftarrow e_1 \,\&\, \cdots \,\&\, \wild \leftarrow
e_m)\, \out{\ok}{\nil}$. Each race fixes one truth value; a clause
signals only if one of its literals was made true; the join fires only if
every clause signalled. $P_\varphi$ can succeed iff $\varphi$ is
satisfiable.
\end{proof}

So the specimen's universal negative is as hard as any $\coNP$ problem,
and the hardness is entirely in the races: no run is long. The scientist
who could choose the resolution would need one experiment to confirm
success. Confirming its absence is the problem.

\subsection{The same problem, stated by bisimulation}\label{sec:normaliser}

The asymmetry has a bisimulation-native form, which is how it was first
reached and which shows that the question is native to the calculus
rather than imported.

\begin{definition}[Explicit bounded processes]
An \emph{explicit bounded process} is a finite acyclic labelled
transition system $G = (S, \Sigma, \to, s_0)$, given as a list of
transitions. $G$ is \emph{determinate} if every state has at most one
$a$-successor for each $a$. $\sbisim$ is strong bisimilarity and $\treq$
trace equivalence.
\end{definition}

\begin{construction}[Encoding]\label{con:encoding}
With pairwise distinct closed names $\go_s, \lk_s$ for states and $a$
for labels,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{E}(s) &\;=\; \out{\lk_s}{\nil}
  \ \mid\ \prod_{(s,a,t) \in \to}
     \mathsf{for}(\wild \leftarrow a \,\&\, \wild \leftarrow \lk_s)\,
     \out{\go_t}{\nil},\\
\mathcal{E}(G) &\;=\; \out{\go_{s_0}}{\nil}
  \ \mid\ \prod_{s \in S} \inp{\wild}{\go_s}{\mathcal{E}(s)},
\end{aligned}
\]
observed by $\Obs_\Sigma = \{\hole \mid \out{a}{\nil} : a \in \Sigma\}$.
The lock $\lk_s$ lets one outgoing transition of $s$ fire; a shared state
is encoded once. Communications on $\go$-names are administrative and
deterministic; taking transitions to be principal steps that consume the
offered $\out{a}{\nil}$, followed by
administrative normalisation, $s \sbisim t$ iff $\mathcal{E}(s)
\bisim_{\Obs_\Sigma} \mathcal{E}(t)$, and likewise for traces.
\end{construction}

On explicit bounded processes, $\sbisim$ is decidable in polynomial time
\cite{PaigeTarjan87} ($\Pclass$-complete on finite systems in general
\cite{BGS92}), while $\treq$ is $\coNP$-complete, by way of the
inequivalence of regular expressions built from union and concatenation
only \cite{StockmeyerMeyer73}, with an end marker to handle prefix-closed
traces.
Branching time is cheap; linear time is not.

\begin{definition}[Normaliser]
A \emph{normaliser} is a polynomial-time map $\Phi$ on explicit bounded
processes with $G \treq H \iff \Phi(G) \sbisim \Phi(H)$. It is
\emph{faithful} if $\Phi(G) \treq G$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:normaliser}
$\Pclass = \NPclass$ if and only if a normaliser exists, and then a
faithful one exists. Equivalently: the projection $\pi : B/{\sbisim} \to
B/{\treq}$ has a polynomial-time section.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
($\Leftarrow$) Decide $G \treq H$ by testing $\Phi(G) \sbisim \Phi(H)$;
this puts a $\coNP$-complete problem in $\Pclass$. ($\Rightarrow$) If
$\Pclass = \NPclass$ the polynomial hierarchy collapses to $\Pclass$. Let
$m(G)$ be the least size of a process trace-equivalent to $G$, and let
$\Phi(G)$ be the lexicographically least such process of size $m(G)$,
over states numbered $0, \dots, m$ and the labels occurring in traces of
$G$. Both depend only on the trace class. Its bits are found by
$\Sigma^p_2$ questions (there is a $G'$ extending a given prefix such
that, for all traces up to the depth bound, membership agrees), hence in
polynomial time. Then $\Phi$ is faithful, equal trace classes give equal
normal forms, and $\Phi(G) \sbisim \Phi(H)$ gives $\Phi(G) \treq
\Phi(H)$ and so $G \treq H$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Faithful determinate normalisers do not exist]
\label{prop:determinate}
There are $G_n$ with $|G_n| = O(n^2)$ such that every determinate $H
\treq G_n$ has at least $2^n$ states.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $G_n$ have the traces of the words of $L_n = \{uv \in \{0,1\}^{2n} :
u \neq v,\ |u| = |v| = n\}$, each followed by $\ok$: it guesses a
position, records a bit, counts $n$ further and checks the bit differs,
with states indexed by position, guessed index and bit. For determinate
$H \treq G_n$ each $u \in \{0,1\}^n$ leads to a unique state $h_u$, and
for $u \neq u'$ the trace $u'\ok$ is available from $h_u$ but not from
$h_{u'}$. This is the blow-up of determinisation
\cite{MeyerFischer71,Moore71}.
\end{proof}

Without faithfulness a determinate normaliser exists iff $\Pclass =
\NPclass$: spell out the bits of the canonical form of
Theorem~\ref{thm:normaliser} as a straight line. So the type of a
normaliser is a \emph{section of the coarsening projection}, choosing one
bisimilarity class inside each trace class, and the question is whether
such a choice can be made cheaply. A choice that lands in determinate
processes is provably expensive.

\subsection{Internalising the experimenter}\label{sec:internal}

Inside the calculus the experimenter is a process. We write it
$\enc{\Phi}(x)$, a fixed term with a free name $x$ on which it expects its
specimen, and the configuration is
\[
\enc{\Phi}(x) \ \mid\ \out{x}{E} \;\;\to^*\;\; \text{verdict}.
\]
In context-labelled terms the experimenter \emph{is} the context
$C_\Phi = \enc{\Phi}(x) \mid \out{x}{\hole}$, whose hole lies under a
lift. By Remark~\ref{rem:bihol} this is where bisimilarity fails to be a
congruence: invariance of the verdict under $\treq$ is a burden the
experimenter carries, not a property it inherits.

\begin{lemma}[What a received name is good for]\label{lem:uses}
In $\inp{y}{x}{R}$ every free occurrence of $y$ in $R$ is the subject of
an input or output or the argument of a drop. So, in pure rho, once
$\enc{\Phi}(x)$ has received $\quo{E}$ it can (i) run $E$, by a drop;
(ii) use $\quo{E}$ as a channel, which tests it for name equality against
names it or its environment constructs; or (iii) forward it, deferring
(i) or (ii).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Names occur only as subjects, binders, under drop, and beneath quotes,
where they are not free.
\end{proof}

So in pure rho every test on a specimen's code is an interaction. What
separates a held specimen from a live one is \emph{resource}: interaction
with a live $E$ can use it up, while a name can be dropped as often as the
experimenter pays for. The \texttt{where}-clause variant adds the
scientist's third grade: a guard may evaluate a name predicate on
$\quo{E}$ without running it.

\begin{example}[Why one experiment is not enough]\label{ex:linear}
Let $G = a.(b + c)$ and $H = a.b + a.c$, so $G \treq H$ and $G
\not\sbisim H$. An experimenter interacting with a live $\mathcal{E}(H)$
learns nothing until it offers $a$, and the join that consumes the offer
also chooses the branch. The losing branch survives only as a dead join on
a lock no observer in $\Obs_\Sigma$ can name. A faithful normal form must
have both $ab$ and $ac$, and a single experiment cannot recover the trace
it did not see.
\end{example}

\subsection{Access tiers are structural rules}\label{sec:tiers}

Quotation gives the specimen the exponential of linear logic: $\quo{E}$
behaves as $!E$, the drop as dereliction, the duplicator as contraction.
The scientist's grades of access become tiers distinguished by which of
these rules the experimenter may use, and whether it may read.

\begin{definition}[Tiers]\label{def:tiers}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \emph{Linear} (destructive assay): the received name is dropped at
most once, as in the exactly-once input $\mathsf{for}(y
\mathrel{\circ\!\!-} x)P$. No contraction. Type $B \multimap B$.
\item \emph{Interaction} (black box): the received name is used only under
drop, and forwarded only to receivers that do likewise; the experimenter's
contexts mention, of the specimen's names, only a declared public
interface. Contraction is allowed; reading and name guessing are not.
Type ${!}B \multimap B$ with $!$ extensional.
\item \emph{Reflection} (white box): as (b), and in addition the
experimenter may evaluate name predicates on the received name --- on
the quoted code, which does not run while it is read. Type $@B
\rightarrow B$, with $@$ an intensional box whose eliminators may inspect
code.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

A grading (\S\ref{sec:fuzzy}) applied to a specimen in transit belongs
to the interaction tier only if it is injective and blind to the
structure of the specimen's names; a grading that computes on that
structure is a reflection-tier act, whoever chooses it
(\S\ref{sec:covert}).

\begin{conjecture}[Drop-only usage is extensional]\label{conj:dropOnly}
If the hole of $C$ lies under a lift and the corresponding name is used
drop-only, then $P \bisim_\Obs Q$ implies $C[P] \bisim_\Obs C[Q]$ for
suitable $\Obs$: $@$ restricted to dereliction and contraction is $!$.
\end{conjecture}

\subsection{Experimental regimes}\label{sec:regimes}

\begin{definition}[Regime]\label{def:regime}
An \emph{experimental regime} is a tuple $\Reg = (A, I, O, c)$: an access
tier $A$; an intervention language $I$, a set of partial resolutions the
experimenter may impose on a copy of the specimen (realised, where they
are realised at all, by the knockouts of \S\ref{sec:fuzzy}); a readout discipline $O$ (single,
pooled, or graded, read through the assay trichotomy); and the cost
model $c$ of \S\ref{sec:cost}. A \emph{copy} is released by one
communication that applies its signature and intervention, and is
identified with it. An \emph{experiment} is a finite pool of copies run
against test contexts allowed by $A$. $\enc{\Phi}(x)$ \emph{exploits}
$\Reg$ for $\ABS$ if, for every flat $E$, the configuration
$\enc{\Phi}(x) \mid \out{x}{E}$ performs only experiments of $\Reg$, runs
at phlogiston polynomial in $|E|$, and reports, independently of the
scheduler, whether $E$ can succeed.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Copies cost]\label{lem:copies}
Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:acct}, a run of phlogiston $c$ releases at most
$c$ copies.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each copy is its own communication, charged at least $1$.
\end{proof}

Unstamped duplicates are cheaper --- one communication can deliver
$\quo{E}$ to a body with many drops --- but they share channels, are
neither isolated nor attributable, and carry no intervention; under the
adversary below they contribute nothing.

\begin{definition}[Interaction regime]\label{def:blackbox}
$\Reg$ is an \emph{interaction regime} if its tier is (a) or (b) and the
outcome of each experiment on $E$ is determined by the experiment and by
whether each copy, under its intervention, succeeds at its stamped
interface. Interventions are granted as a primitive over declared race
ports. This is more than such an experimenter could otherwise do: the
listeners of a race share a subject, so separating them needs a grade that
discriminates on the structure of their continuations, which belongs on
the reflection side (\S\ref{sec:covert}). Likewise success or failure
of a copy is granted as an observation, although by
\cite[Prop.~Asymmetry]{Scientist} a failure is in general only $\bot$;
flat copies are bounded, and we grant quiescence detection. Both grants
only strengthen what follows. Races an intervention leaves unresolved
are resolved by a scheduler outside the experimenter's control, and
correctness is required under every scheduler.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Interaction cannot certify universal negatives]
\label{thm:blackbox}
For each $d$ there are flat $E_\bot$ and $E_\sigma$, $\sigma \in
\{0,1\}^d$, of size $O(d \log d)$, such that $E_\bot$ cannot succeed,
each $E_\sigma$ can, and every $\enc{\Phi}(x)$ that, using an interaction
regime, reports correctly and scheduler-independently on all of them
releases at least $2^d$ intervened copies on $E_\bot$; under
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:acct} its phlogiston there is at least $2^d$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathit{Race}_i = \out{b_i}{\nil} \mid
\inp{\wild}{b_i}{\out{e_i^0}{\nil}} \mid
\inp{\wild}{b_i}{\out{e_i^1}{\nil}}$, and put
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_\sigma &= \prod_{i \le d} \mathit{Race}_i \ \mid\
  \mathsf{for}(\wild \leftarrow e_1^{\sigma_1} \,\&\, \cdots \,\&\,
  \wild \leftarrow e_d^{\sigma_d})\, \out{\ok}{\nil},\\
E_\bot &= \prod_{i \le d} \mathit{Race}_i \ \mid\
  \mathsf{for}(\wild \leftarrow e_1^0 \,\&\, \cdots \,\&\,
  \wild \leftarrow e_d^0 \,\&\, \wild \leftarrow z)\, \out{\ok}{\nil},
\end{aligned}
\]
with $z$ a name nothing sends on and all names distinct (quoted binary
numerals give the size bound). $E_\sigma$ succeeds exactly under the resolution
$\sigma$; $E_\bot$ never does. A copy with intervention $\tau$ fails on
$E_\bot$, and fails on $E_\sigma$ unless $\tau$ is the full assignment
$\sigma$: if $\tau$ disagrees with $\sigma$ the join cannot fire, and if
$\tau$ leaves a race open a scheduler resolving it against $\sigma$ makes
the copy fail. Fix such a scheduler. Every experiment then returns the
same outcome on $E_\sigma$ as on $E_\bot$ unless it contains a copy with
full intervention $\sigma$. Run $\enc{\Phi}(x)$ on $E_\bot$; it reports
failure. If some $\sigma$ was never applied, then on $E_\sigma$ every
experiment returns what it returned on $E_\bot$, the run makes the same
adaptive choices by induction, and it wrongly reports failure. So all
$2^d$ full interventions were applied, and Lemma~\ref{lem:copies} gives
the cost bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nobb}
No interaction regime is exploitable for $\ABS$; in
particular the linear regime --- one specimen, one measurement --- is
not.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Why this says nothing about $\Pclass$ versus $\NPclass$]
\label{rem:oracle}
With reflection the family is trivial: $\enc{\Phi}$ reads the final join
and sees either the conjunct on $z$ or the hidden $\sigma$. The bound is
purely about access. It is a query lower bound for unstructured search,
the in-calculus analog of the \emph{separating} half of the
Baker--Gill--Solovay theorem \cite{BGS75}: an interaction-only scientist
computes with the specimen as an oracle. We give no analog of the
collapsing half, so the correspondence with relativisation is an
analogy.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Reflection is exactly as hard as $\Pclass$ versus $\NPclass$]
\label{thm:whitebox}
Under Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:sim}--\ref{hyp:refl}, $\Pclass = \NPclass$ if
and only if some regime is exploitable for $\ABS$; when one is, a
reflection regime is.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
($\Rightarrow$) $\ABS \in \Pclass$, decided by some $M$. A reflection
regime reconstructs $E$ from $\quo{E}$ by adaptive structural guards: at
each node of the term it asks which term former is at the root and
compares subject names against names it has already reconstructed, a
polynomial number of guards each of polynomial size, re-sending
$\quo{E}$ to itself between queries (sketched). By
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:refl} this costs polynomial phlogiston; it then runs
$R_M$ on the reconstruction (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:sim}). No copy of $E$
runs, so the report is scheduler-independent. ($\Leftarrow$) The report of an exploiting
$\enc{\Phi}$ is scheduler-independent, so simulating any one schedule
computes it; the run costs polynomial phlogiston, so by
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:acct} a deterministic machine simulates it in
polynomial time, and $\ABS \in \Pclass$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The problem, in the frame]\label{cor:restatement}
Under the hypotheses of \S\ref{sec:cost}:
\begin{quote}
$\Pclass \neq \NPclass$ if and only if no mortal scientist, in any
experimental regime, can certify uniformly, at cost polynomial in the
size of the specimen, that a flat specimen cannot reach a designated
state.
\end{quote}
Interaction regimes provably cannot (Theorem~\ref{thm:blackbox}). The
remaining content of $\Pclass \neq \NPclass$ is therefore a statement
about reflection: that holding a specimen's code affords no laboratory
for its universal negatives. A proof of $\Pclass = \NPclass$ would
exhibit one.
\end{corollary}

Theorem~\ref{thm:whitebox} is a restatement, not a technique. What the
frame adds is a vocabulary in which the parts of the restatement --- the
tier, the intervention language, the readout, the cost model --- can be
varied one at a time. The next two sections take stock of the tools for
varying them.

\section{Tool I: fuzzy rho, and knocking out pieces of the code}\label{sec:fuzzy}
% =====================================================================

Repetition alone yields only existential evidence. If a copy of the
specimen succeeds, the specimen can succeed; if it fails, the scheduler
may simply have resolved a race the other way. A biologist facing the same
problem knocks genes out: removes one component, holds the rest fixed, and
compares with a control. Fuzzy rho supplies the corresponding apparatus,
because in it the communication rule itself modulates the code being
sent, and the experimenter controls the modulation by choosing where to
send.

\subsection{Graded communication}

Fuzzy rho \cite{FuzzyRho} adds an action of grades on processes and a
comparison of interaction surfaces,
\[
(-\act-)\colon A\times\Proc\to\Proc, \qquad
(-\cmp-)\colon \Names\times\Names\to A,
\]
with $A$ a quantale and a monotone measure $\mu : \Proc \to A$, and
replaces strict channel equality by a graded rule
\[
\inp{y}{x}{R} \ \mid\ \out{u}{Q} \;\to\; R\{\quo{((u \cmp x) \act Q)}/y\}.
\]
The \emph{order axiom} requires $a_1 \le a_2 \Rightarrow \mu(a_1 \act P)
\le \mu(a_2 \act P)$: a larger grade transmits at least as much. The
action is a parameter of the calculus, constrained only by this axiom.

The minimal model of \cite{FuzzyRho} takes grades to be names, $u \cmp x =
\quo{(\dr u \mid \dr x)}$ (where $\dr z$ is the process quoted by $z$),
and an action that re-channels every free name of the transmitted term,
\[
(u \cmp x) \act Q = Q\{\quo{(\dr{u} \mid \dr{x} \mid \dr{z})}/z : z \in \fn(Q)\}.
\]
This \emph{accumulator} never deletes: it stamps every channel of $Q$ with
the match signature of the redex, and the order axiom holds in it modulo a
congruence side-condition \cite{FuzzyRho}. Its dual, the
\emph{attenuator}, replaces $\mid$ by the multiset meet $\wedge$, so that
channels can collapse. Neither deletes, but the calculus does not require
an action to preserve terms, and other models can.

\subsection{Knockouts}

In fuzzy rho any input may react with any output, at the grade that
compares their channels. That is the experimenter's dial. To knock a piece
out of $Q$ before $\inp{y}{x}{R}$ receives it, the experimenter sends $Q$
not on $x$ but on a channel $u$ chosen so that $(u \cmp x) \act Q$ is $Q$
with that piece removed. The mismatch between $u$ and $x$ is the
treatment.

Three conditions on a model make this an apparatus.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(K\arabic*),leftmargin=2.6em]
\item \emph{A deleting action.} Some grades remove parts of the term they
act on.
\item \emph{A unit control.} $x \cmp x = \unit$ and $\unit \act Q = Q$, so
a copy sent on the receiver's own channel is the untreated baseline.
The minimal model fails this: there $x \cmp x = \quo{(\dr x \mid \dr x)}$,
a watermark rather than the identity.
\item \emph{A bottom.} $u \cmp x = \bot$ for most mismatched pairs, and
the rule does not fire at $\bot$. Otherwise a copy's own internal
communications are graded too, and it ceases to be a faithful copy.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{construction}[The deletion model]\label{con:deletion}
Call a \emph{piece} of $Q$ either a $\mid$-prime component of $Q$ or of the
body of one of its prefixes --- a prefix together with its continuation
--- not beneath a lift or a quote; or a single binding $y_i \leftarrow
x_i$ of a join. A grade is $\bot$ or a set $\Phi$ of structural formulae,
and $\Phi \act Q$ deletes every piece of $Q$ satisfying some formula in
$\Phi$, the formulae being evaluated on the pieces of $Q$ itself and the
outermost deletion taking precedence (a deleted prefix piece becomes
$\nil$; a deleted binding is dropped from its join). Order grades by reverse inclusion, with $\bot$ adjoined
below, and take union as product; since union distributes over
intersections this is a quantale with unit $\unit = \varnothing$. The
comparison is $x \cmp x = \unit$; $u \cmp x = \Phi$ when $u$ is an
\emph{experimental channel} for $x$ carrying $\Phi$ --- a name built from
$\dr x$, a code for $\Phi$, and an unforgeable component held by the
experimenter; and $\bot$ otherwise. Since $\Phi_1 \supseteq \Phi_2$
deletes every position $\Phi_2$ deletes, $\Phi_1 \act Q$ is obtained from
$\Phi_2 \act Q$ by deleting further pieces. So the order axiom holds with
$\mu$ taking a term to its quotation ordered by deletion of pieces, just
as the minimal model of \cite{FuzzyRho} takes $\mu(P) = \quo{P}$ under a
precongruence on names: a smaller grade deletes more.
\end{construction}

\noindent
As stated in \cite{FuzzyRho} the measure lands in the grade quantale;
here it lands in names ordered by deletion, as in the minimal model, where
grades and names coincide. Whether a single quantale can carry both is
part of open problem~7.

The unforgeable component ensures that no two of the specimen's own
channels form an experimental pair, so inside a copy only exact matches
fire and the copy runs as ordinary rho (K3). A grade made only of formulae
picking out prefix pieces is a \emph{prefix-deleting} grade. One that
picks out bindings is a \emph{weakening} grade, because it relaxes a
synchronisation. The distinction matters.

\begin{lemma}[Prefix deletion is run-subset]\label{lem:runsubset}
Let $\Phi$ be prefix-deleting. If $\Phi \act Q \to^* R$, then $Q \to^* R
\mid D$ for some $D$; in particular if $\Phi \act Q$ can succeed, so can
$Q$, by a run that resolves every race the same way.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A deleted prefix piece is a parallel component of $Q$ or of a body in
$Q$, so $Q$ differs from $\Phi \act Q$ by components that are released
as their enclosing prefixes fire. By induction on the run, each term
reached from $\Phi \act Q$ is matched by a term reached from $Q$ that adds
the released pieces in parallel, with the same substitutions applied.
Reduction is closed under parallel composition, so extra components never
disable a step. Pieces beneath a lift are excluded because a transmitted
term may later be run or used as a name, and deleting inside it would
change what the receiver does.
\end{proof}

\noindent
So a prefix-deleting knockout cannot manufacture success: positive
evidence from a knocked-out copy is evidence about the specimen. A
weakening grade has no such guarantee. Dropping a binding from a join lets
it fire with less than the specimen requires, which creates runs the
specimen does not have; \S\ref{sec:covert} shows what that buys.

\subsection{Isolation and attribution: the stamp}

Copies run side by side must not interfere, and each observation must be
traceable to its copy. The accumulator's stamp does this, and can be
composed with a deletion grade: the experimenter's grade for a copy is a
deletion set together with a signature.

\begin{definition}[Signature, stamp]
A \emph{signature} is a closed process $S$ having, as a $\mid$-prime
component, an inert input $K_S = \inp{\wild}{k_S}{\nil}$ on an
unforgeable name $k_S$ occurring nowhere else. The \emph{stamp}
$\theta_S$ sends each name $z$ in normal form to $\quo{(S \mid \dr{z})}$,
and $S \act P$ applies $\theta_S$ to the names of $P$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Stamps are injective]\label{lem:injective}
$\theta_S(z_1) \nequiv \theta_S(z_2)$ implies $z_1 \nequiv z_2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$S \mid \dr{z}$ has $K_S$ as a component, so it is never $\equiv$ to a
lone drop and only the first clause of $\nequiv$ applies. Up to $\equiv$
a process is a finite multiset of $\mid$-prime components, and the free
commutative monoid is cancellative.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Distinct signatures are disjoint]\label{lem:disjoint}
If the names $k_S$ are globally unique, $k_{S_1} \not\nequiv k_{S_2}$, and
$z_1, z_2$ are names of a specimen that mentions neither, then
$\theta_{S_1}(z_1) \not\nequiv \theta_{S_2}(z_2)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $S_1 \mid \dr{z_1} \equiv S_2 \mid \dr{z_2}$, the prime component
$K_{S_1}$ occurs on the right, in $S_2$ or in $\dr{z_2}$, neither of
which mentions $k_{S_1}$.
\end{proof}

Copies with distinct signatures therefore share no channel and cannot
interfere, and every observation carries the signature of the copy that
made it. The experimenter knows each signature, so it can compute the
stamped interface on which to listen.

\subsection{Fidelity: inertness and equivariance}

A stamped copy is useful only if it is a faithful copy. If $E$ contains
$\drp{z}$, the copy contains $\drp{\theta_S(z)}$, which runs $S \mid
\dr{z}$; signatures must be inert. And flat substitution stamps the names
a term has when it is sent, not the names it computes while it runs.

\begin{example}[Flat stamping is not equivariant]\label{ex:nonequivariant}
Let $P = \out{a}{\nil} \mid \inp{y}{a}{\out{y}{\nil}} \mid
\inp{\wild}{\quo{\nil}}{\out{\ok}{\nil}}$. Then $P$ receives $y :=
\quo{\nil}$, outputs on $\quo{\nil}$, and succeeds. In $S \act P$ the
names $a$, $\ok$ and $\quo{\nil}$ are stamped, but the transmitted
$\quo{\nil}$ is synthesised from the object of a lift that has no names,
and is not. The copy outputs on $\quo{\nil}$ while its listener waits on
$\theta_S(\quo{\nil}) \nequiv \quo{S}$, and cannot succeed.
\end{example}

\begin{definition}[Experimental grading]\label{def:expgrading}
A grading is \emph{experimental} if its signatures are signatures in the
sense above, pairwise distinct across copies (E1); inert (E2); and the
stamp is \emph{equivariant}: $P \to P'$ implies $S \act P \to S \act P'$,
and every reduction of $S \act P$ is of this form (E3).
\end{definition}

We read (E3) as a run-time analog of the binder transport law $a \act
[y]P = [y](a' \act P)$ of \cite{FuzzyRho}: the copy runs inside the stamp.
It fails in general, by Example~\ref{ex:nonequivariant}. Under
(E1)--(E3) the stamp is a bisimulation between $P$ and $S \act P$ with
observations on $z$ matched to observations on $\theta_S(z)$. Flat
specimens synthesise no channel names and contain no drops, so flat
stamping is experimental on them.

\subsection{Intervention, control, dose and readout}

\paragraph{Intervention.} Deleting every losing listener of a race
resolves it: this is Pearl's $\mathrm{do}(\cdot)$ \cite{Pearl09},
performed by choosing the channel on which the specimen is sent. A
\emph{full intervention} resolves every race, and under it a flat copy is
confluent. By Lemma~\ref{lem:runsubset}, if the copy succeeds, the
specimen succeeds under that resolution. The listeners of a race share a
subject, so a grade that deletes one and spares the other must
discriminate on the structure of their continuations. The experimenter
never inspects the code; the comparison does it on the experimenter's
behalf. That is legitimate, but it places a structurally selective grade
on the reflection side of the ledger (\S\ref{sec:covert}).

\paragraph{Control.} A copy sent on the receiver's own channel is
received at the unit grade, which acts as the identity (K2): the untreated
baseline.

\paragraph{Dose.} The order axiom says a larger grade transmits at least
as much, as measured by $\mu$; in the deletion model, a larger grade
deletes less. Read as a dose--response law it licenses inference from
graded treatments, but only for outcomes monotone in that measure, which
is a further assumption.

\paragraph{Readout.} A collapsing grade applied to the experimenter's own
channels merges the success channels of a pool onto one name, so that one
readout reports whether any member succeeded. Given quiescence detection,
a negative pooled readout certifies failure for every member; without it,
a negative readout is $\bot$. Pooling saves readouts, not runs.

\paragraph{What knockouts buy.} Under a full intervention, and given
quiescence detection, a failure is conclusive for that intervention.
Knockouts convert demonic scheduling into the experimenter's choice:
success of a specimen is the existence of an intervention under which a
copy succeeds --- an $\NPclass$ witness, chosen rather than granted ---
and failure is failure under every intervention. The universal negative
becomes a problem of \emph{experimental design}: can polynomially many
knockout experiments cover the space of resolutions?

\section{Tool II: the rho combinators, and where the complexity lives}\label{sec:comb}
% =====================================================================

The rho combinators \cite{RhoComb} translate rho into a name-free
calculus of atoms, each doing one job. Including or excluding atoms gives
a lattice of calculi \cite[\S9]{RhoComb}, following Yoshida
\cite{Yoshida02}. Because each atom isolates one structural operation,
the lattice is a literal map of where the complexity of the scientist's
problem lives.

\subsection{Atoms and rules}

Atoms take names; payloads are written as names in presentation~A of
\cite{RhoComb}. The rules are:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\cdd(a,b,c) \mid \cm(a,v) \to \cm(b,v) \mid \cm(c,v) & \text{distribute}\\
\ckk(a) \mid \cm(a,v) \to \nil & \text{kill}\\
\cfw(a,b) \mid \cm(a,v) \to \cm(b,v) & \text{forward}\\
\cbl(a,b) \mid \cm(a,v) \to \cfw(v,b) & \text{bind left}\\
\cbr(a,b) \mid \cm(a,v) \to \cfw(b,v) & \text{bind right}\\
\csy(a,b,c) \mid \cm(a,v) \to \cfw(b,c) & \text{synchronise}\\
\cfw(a,b) \mid \cq(a,p) \to \cm(b,\quo{p}) & \text{quote}\\
\cev(a) \mid \cm(a,\quo{p}) \to p & \text{evaluate}\\
\ccons_A(a_1,\dots,a_n,f) \mid \cm(a_1,v_1) \mid \cdots \mid \cm(a_n,v_n)
  \to \cm(f,\quo{A(v_1,\dots,v_n)}) & \text{construct}
\end{array}
\]
with $\cm$ the message, $\cq$ a store that only a forwarder can read, and
one constructor per atom shape. The translation from rho is linear in
size up to a logarithmic factor from its second phase
\cite[Prop.~linear]{RhoComb}. We assume its run-time overhead is
polynomial, which the source states for the direction from rho to the
combinators; the results of \S\ref{sec:joins} are proved directly on
combinator terms and do not depend on transfer.

Three invariants of \cite{RhoComb} carry the lattice.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Name closure.} Every name in a reduct lies in the closure of
the term's names under the shapes of the constructors present.
\item \emph{Weighted atom count.} Weighting $\cbl, \cbr, \csy$ at $2$ and
other non-message atoms at $1$, every rule but $\cev$ strictly decreases
weight. So without $\cev$ every run has length at most twice the number
of non-message atoms.
\item \emph{Message count.} $\cdd$ is the only rule that increases the
number of messages without opening a payload.
\end{itemize}
The constructor axis is a Boolean lattice; $\cev$ is a strict edge; and
the conjecture that the calculus without $\cdd$ is strongly normalising
even with $\cev$ and all constructors is open \cite[Conj.~dup]{RhoComb}.

\subsection{The atoms are structural rules}

atom & linear-logic reading & role \\
\midrule

$\ckk$ & weakening & discards a message \\
$\cev$ & dereliction (counit) & opens a name by running it \\

One operation is missing: a destructor of names that descends without

\[
\cm(a, \quo{A(v_1,\dots,v_n)}) \mid \cuncons_A(a, b_1, \dots, b_n) \;\to\;

presented as a case split over shapes so that a mismatch does not create a

\cite{ObserverExt} and of the scientist's reflection. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:wf}, a specimen's internal names lie below
$\quo{E}$ in the quote-depth order: constructors climb, $\cev$ descends by
running, and $\cuncons$ descends without running. Reading is exactly
downward motion that does not execute.

\subsection{Where the hardness of universal negatives lives}\label{sec:joins}

Run $\ABS$ on combinator terms: decide that no run delivers a message at
$\ok$.

\begin{proposition}[Join-free atoms are easy]\label{prop:nl}
For terms over $\cm$ and atoms from $\{\cfw, \ckk, \cdd, \cq\}$, $\ABS$ is
in $\mathbf{NL}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each rule consumes one message and one atom and produces only messages;
no atom is created. So the ancestry of a message at $\ok$ is a chain of
single-input steps, which is a path in the static graph with an edge
$a \to b$ for each $\cfw(a,b)$ and edges $a \to b$, $a \to c$ for each
$\cdd(a,b,c)$ (a $\cq$ is a message that only $\cfw$ may move). A simple
path uses distinct atoms, and no other message can help or hinder. So
reachability of $\ok$ is graph reachability, and its complement is in
$\mathbf{coNL} = \mathbf{NL}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Synchronisation is enough]\label{thm:sy}
$\ABS$ is $\coNP$-complete for terms over $\{\cm, \csy\}$, and hence for
$\{\cm, \cfw, \csy\}$, although every run has length at most twice the
number of atoms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Membership: runs are linear by the weighted atom count. Hardness: read
$\cfw(a,b)$ as the one-shot rule $a \multimap b$ and $\csy(a,b,c)$ as the
one-shot rule $a \otimes b \multimap c$ --- a message at $a$ creates
$\cfw(b,c)$, which then needs a message at $b$. Given a CNF over
$v_1,\dots,v_n$:
\begin{itemize}
\item for each occurrence of a literal in clause $j$, a resource message
$\cm(R)$ and the rule $\cfw(R, L_j)$;
\item for each variable $x$, one message $\cm(X_x)$ racing between
$\cfw(X_x, T_0)$ and $\cfw(X_x, F_0)$;
\item a $T$-chain $\csy(T_{i-1}, R_i, T_i)$ over the resources of the
\emph{negative} occurrences of $x$, ending in $\cfw(T_k, G_x)$, and an
$F$-chain over the positive ones;
\item a chain of $\csy$ joining all $G_x$ and all $L_j$ and ending at
$\ok$.
\end{itemize}
Only one chain per variable completes, since there is one $X_x$, and
completing it consumes every resource of the opposite literal; a clause
can then be signalled only by a literal consistent with the chosen
assignment. So $\ok$ is reachable iff the CNF is satisfiable, and the term
is linear in its size. A static $\cfw(a,b)$ is $\csy(z,a,b) \mid \cm(z)$
with $z$ fresh.
\end{proof}

The construction over $\{\cm, \cfw, \csy\}$ was checked by exhaustive
state-space search against a truth-table SAT check on 920 random CNFs,
with complete agreement: 800 instances with up to three variables and
four clauses (seeds 1 and 7), and 120 with two variables and three to
five clauses (seed 5); 764 satisfiable and 156 unsatisfiable in all. The
reduction to $\{\cm, \csy\}$ rests on the confluence of $\csy(z,a,b) \mid
\cm(z)$, which has a single consumer and adds one step per forwarder. It is a one-shot instance of Kanovich's theorem that the Horn
fragment of linear logic is $\NPclass$-complete \cite{Kanovich92}: the
fragment over $\{\cm, \cfw, \csy\}$ is one-shot affine Horn logic with
clauses $a \multimap b$ and $a \otimes b \multimap c$, and single-output
clauses already suffice. Fan-out is not needed because the input pays in
advance, one resource per literal occurrence, and consistency is enforced
by \emph{consumption} rather than broadcast.

The dichotomy extends along the atoms that synchronise.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
atom & why it is a join & status \\
\midrule
$\csy$ & produces $\cfw(b,c)$: $a \otimes b \multimap c$ & proved, checked \\
$\ccons_A$, arity $\ge 2$ & consumes $n$ messages at once & immediate \\
$\cbl$, $\cbr$ & produce a forwarder at a payload & sketched \\
$\cev$ & a payload carrying $\cfw(b,c)$ releases an edge & sketched \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
For $\cbl$, $\cbr$ and $\cev$ the encoding must carry the variable's
choice in payloads, since forwarding preserves a payload; the gadget is
routine but not yet written out.

\begin{quote}
\itshape The hardness of universal negatives lives in synchronisation ---
the tensor on the left of the Horn arrow. Duplication alone adds none.
\end{quote}

\noindent
Evaluation and multi-input construction are not exceptions to this: they
are hard exactly because they can release or perform a join.

\subsection{Where the cost of an experimenter lives: sharing}\label{sec:sharing}

The other half of the picture is the experimenter's. Asperti proved that
every simply typed $\lambda$-term normalises in a number of Lévy
\emph{families} of reductions polynomial in its size, and concluded that
$\Pclass = \NPclass$ ``up to sharing'' \cite{AspertiSharing}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The optimal root.} $\eta$-expanding each shared higher-type
variable once, before reduction, leaves every sharing node (fan) of
Lamping's graphs \cite{Lamping90} at atomic type, so fans never meet a
$\lambda$ or an application. The number of $\beta$-steps is then bounded
by the size of the graph, all logical steps can fire before any
structural one, and what remains is fan duplication and annihilation.
\item \emph{SAT.} The encoding is deterministic enumeration: a step
function iterated $2^n$ times by $n$-fold composition of the Church
numeral $\mathit{two}$. Up to sharing it costs a number of families
linear in the size of the formula and quadratic in $n$.
\item \emph{Where the cost goes.} On the BOHM implementation, for the
family $P_n$ of conjunctions of $n$ distinct atoms (a trivially
satisfiable test family), the $\beta$-reductions of the optimal root grow
as $10n^2 + 37n + 38$, while total interactions, including the oracle, go
$374, 1453, 4549, 15206, 55113, 208549$ for $n = 1, \dots, 6$
\cite{AspertiSharing}.
\item \emph{The corollary.} If $\Pclass \neq \NPclass$, the overhead of
optimal sharing cannot in general be polynomial in the number of families
\cite[Prop.~5.1]{AspertiSharing}; compare the non-elementary lower bound
of \cite{AspertiMairson98}.
\end{itemize}

In linear-logic terms the split is between \emph{logical} work (cuts,
here communications) and \emph{structural} work, and among the structural
rules the cost is in contraction commuting with contraction across box
levels, together with the bookkeeping --- Lamping's oracle --- that tells
paired fans from unpaired ones. On our reading, exchange and weakening
contribute nothing.

Rho needs no optimal root to make contraction cheap. A doubling process,
receiving a quoted term and sending back two copies, makes $2^d$ copies in
$d$ communications. So:

\begin{quote}
\itshape Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:acct} is Asperti's Proposition~5.1 read in
the other direction. A cost model that charged communications, or shared
bytes, instead of unshared work would be a family-counting model in
disguise, and if Conjecture~\ref{conj:upto} holds, Theorem~\ref{thm:whitebox}
fails in it.
\end{quote}

\noindent
This is a practical point as well as a theoretical one: content-addressed
channels in cost-accounted rho are maximal sharing of code, and the
phlogiston charged for content-addressed data must count unshared
structural work or the metering is family counting.

\section{Combining the tools}\label{sec:combine}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Regimes as points of a product lattice}\label{sec:product}

Sections~\ref{sec:fuzzy} and~\ref{sec:comb} separate two questions the
standard formulation runs together: which atoms the \emph{specimen} is
built from, and which atoms the \emph{experimenter} may use on it.

\paragraph{The specimen axis.} By Proposition~\ref{prop:nl} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:sy}, $\ABS$ is in $\mathbf{NL}$ for specimens without a
synchronising atom, and $\coNP$-complete as soon as $\csy$ (or a
constructor of arity at least two) is present; for $\cbl$, $\cbr$ and
$\cev$ hardness is sketched. The specimen axis measures what a
\emph{reflective} scientist must overcome. It is independent of the
interaction lower bound: routing race $i$ to stage $i+1$ only on outcome
$\sigma_i$ gives a join-free family with the same $2^d$ bound, on which
$\ABS$ is in $\mathbf{NL}$ for anyone who can read the specimen. The
interaction bound is about access; the specimen axis is about
synchronisation.

\paragraph{The experimenter axis.} The tiers of
Definition~\ref{def:tiers} are points:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
tier & experimenter's atoms & status \\
\midrule
linear & $\cev$, no $\cdd$ & one copy (Cor.~\ref{cor:nobb}) \\
interaction & $\cev, \cdd$ & $2^d$ copies (Thm.~\ref{thm:blackbox}) \\
interaction with construction & $\cev, \cdd, \ccons_A$ & see below \\
reflection & $\cev, \cdd, \ccons_A, \cuncons_A$ & iff $\Pclass = \NPclass$
(Thm.~\ref{thm:whitebox}) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
Name closure turns the black-box clause ``no name guessing'' from a
stipulation into a consequence.

\begin{proposition}[Construction does not reach down]\label{prop:closure}
If every internal name of the specimen contains an unforgeable component
outside the closure of the experimenter's names under its constructor
shapes, an experimenter over $\{\cev, \cdd, \ccons_A\}$ never sends or
receives on an internal name of the specimen, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:blackbox} applies to it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By name closure every name the experimenter can produce lies in the
closure of its own names and $\quo{E}$ under its constructor shapes.
Constructors build upward from names present; none descends into
$\quo{E}$, and $\cev$ descends only by running. An internal name with an
unforgeable component outside that closure is therefore never produced.
\end{proof}

\noindent
The adversary of Theorem~\ref{thm:blackbox}, with a demonic scheduler,
goes through for such an experimenter. Assuming Theorem~\ref{thm:whitebox}
transfers to combinator terms, with Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:refl} read for
$\cuncons$, $\Pclass$ versus $\NPclass$ in this frame is the question
whether adding $\cuncons$ at the top of the experimenter's axis buys more
than a polynomial factor against specimens built from $\{\cm, \csy\}$.

\subsection{Knockouts on the combinator image}\label{sec:kocomb}

In combinator form a prefix-deleting knockout deletes an atom, and a
specimen over $\{\cm, \cfw, \csy\}$ is a one-shot Horn program. A
knockout therefore deletes a rule from the program, and an intervention
is a set of deletions: knocking out $\cfw(b_i, e_i^{1})$ is the
intervention $\sigma_i = 0$. This gives the experimental-design problem
of \S\ref{sec:fuzzy} a proof-theoretic reading. Certifying that $\ok$ is
not derivable is certifying non-provability of a Horn sequent, and a
knockout experiment is a case split on which rules a derivation may use.

Two facts shape the design space. Knocking out the losing branch of
every race leaves a confluent term, so the races are the only places an
experiment needs to \emph{choose}. And what makes the remaining question
hard, by Proposition~\ref{prop:nl} and Theorem~\ref{thm:sy}, is
synchronisation, not the number of choices: a join-free specimen with
many races is easy for a reader. This suggests measuring the hardness of
a specimen by its joins rather than its races.

\begin{conjecture}[Joins as the parameter]\label{conj:fpt}
$\ABS$ on specimens over $\{\cm, \cfw, \csy\}$ is fixed-parameter tractable
in the number of $\csy$ atoms.
\end{conjecture}

\noindent
Fixing the set of joins a derivation uses turns the rest into a
unit-capacity flow problem with prescribed demands at join inputs, which
is polynomial. What we do not yet control is circular support, where the
output of one join feeds another's input and vice versa through shared
forwarders. If the conjecture holds, the hardness of universal negatives
is measured exactly by how much a specimen synchronises.

\subsection{Deletion versus weakening: what a grade may do}\label{sec:covert}

A knockout in fuzzy rho is applied in transit by the communication rule,
and the experimenter controls it only by choosing a channel. That makes it
tempting to place every fuzzy experiment in the interaction tier. What a
grade may \emph{do} to the code decides whether that placement is right.

\paragraph{Stamping and prefix deletion are interventions.} The stamp
$z \mapsto \quo{(S \mid \dr z)}$ is injective
(Lemma~\ref{lem:injective}), and a prefix-deleting grade is run-subset
(Lemma~\ref{lem:runsubset}): neither can make a copy succeed where the
specimen could not.

\begin{proposition}[Interventions keep the bound]\label{prop:keep}
Theorem~\ref{thm:blackbox} holds when the interventions of
Definition~\ref{def:blackbox} are any stamping and prefix-deleting grades.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:runsubset}, a copy of $E_\sigma$ that succeeds does so
by a run of $E_\sigma$, which resolves every race as $\sigma$ does. Under
the adversary scheduler any race the copy leaves open is resolved against
$\sigma$, and deleting a winner of $\sigma$ kills success. So a copy
succeeds on $E_\sigma$ only if its grade deletes every loser of $\sigma$
among the race listeners and none of its winners, so distinct $\sigma$
need distinct copies; and every copy fails on $E_\bot$, whose join waits
on $z$.
The counting argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:blackbox} then applies
unchanged.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Weakening is not.} A grade that drops a binding from a join,
or that collapses two channels into one, relaxes a synchronisation. It
creates runs the specimen does not have, and that is exactly what defeats
the interaction bound.

\begin{proposition}[Weakening defeats the interaction bound]\label{prop:collapse}
In the family of Theorem~\ref{thm:blackbox}, each of the following
distinguishes $E_\bot$ from every $E_\sigma$ in one experiment:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.2em,nosep]
\item a grade that drops every binding on an $e$-channel from the final
join and spares the binding on $z$;
\item a grade that collapses $e_i^0$ with $e_i^1$ for every $i$ and keeps
$z$ distinct.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) The join of $E_\sigma$ becomes empty and fires at once; the join of
$E_\bot$ still waits on $z$. (b) Each race delivers on the merged channel
whichever way it resolves, so $E_\sigma$'s join fires under every
resolution, while the binding on $z$ still blocks $E_\bot$.
\end{proof}

\noindent
Weakening does not help in general. On the satisfiability specimen
$P_\varphi$, dropping clause bindings from the final join, or merging
$t_{ij}$ with $f_{ij}$, makes every formula look satisfiable, and the
experiment learns nothing. That is the expected shape: interaction lower
bounds are oracle bounds, and weakening exploits the structure of
particular oracles. But it locates precisely where fuzzy rho can be more
than an apparatus. A weakening grade lets one run stand for many
resolutions; it is a graded aggregate, the fuzzy form of the sharing of
\S\ref{sec:sharing}, and it must be priced as sharing. The combinator
image makes the line literal: deleting an atom is run-subset, while
replacing $\csy(a,b,c)$ by $\cfw(b,c)$ --- dropping the requirement at
$a$ --- is weakening, and Theorem~\ref{thm:sy} says that synchronisation
is exactly where the hardness lives.

\paragraph{Selectivity is reflection.} Independently of what a grade does,
how it chooses matters. A grade that selects pieces by the structure of
their continuations, or merges channels by their shape (as the
attenuator's meet $\dr a \wedge \dr z$ does), computes on the specimen's
code. The experimenter never inspects anything, but the comparison does
so on its behalf, and such a grade belongs on the reflection side of the
ledger. Proposition~\ref{prop:keep} grants structurally selective
interventions anyway, which only strengthens the bound; the line that
matters for the bound is between run-subset grades and weakening ones.

\subsection{Stamps as Lévy labels}\label{sec:levy}

Lévy's labels record the history of a redex, and two redexes are in the
same family when they are copies of one another up to that history
\cite{Levy78}. The accumulator stamp records the matched surfaces of each
communication in every channel of the transmitted term, and stamps nest,
$\quo{(S' \mid S \mid \dr z)}$, once per level. It is a history label of
the same kind. Communications in different stamped copies that differ
only in their stamps are therefore natural candidates for a family.

\begin{conjecture}[Rho is polynomial up to sharing]\label{conj:upto}
Under a cost model that charges one unit per family of stamped
communications, $\ABS$ is decided in polynomially many families: double
the specimen $d$ times, applying at level $i$ the two settings of race
$i$, and read the pool through one collapsed readout.
\end{conjecture}

\noindent
If Conjecture~\ref{conj:upto} holds, Theorem~\ref{thm:whitebox} says
that, unless $\Pclass = \NPclass$, no implementation of family-shared
stamped execution has overhead polynomial in families --- the rho form of
Asperti's Proposition~5.1. The overhead has a concrete home: keeping
$2^d$ copies apart costs stamp size, about $2^d \cdot d$ in total, and
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:acct} charges for exactly that. The bookkeeping that
Lamping's oracle performs reappears as the growth of stamped names.

\subsection{A light namespace logic: the cost hypothesis as a type}
\label{sec:light}

Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:acct} is a stipulation about the runtime. Light logics
show how to make such a stipulation a property of the program: in light
affine logic the functions on binary words typable as $!W \multimap
\S^k W$ are exactly the polynomial-time ones
\cite{Girard98,AspertiLAL98}, and polynomiality is obtained by restricting
contraction under $!$ through stratification. The two Asperti results are
mirror images: unrestricted contraction with hidden cost gives
$\Pclass = \NPclass$ up to sharing; stratified contraction with honest
cost gives exactly $\Pclass$.

Rho is already stratified. By Proposition~\ref{prop:wf} a process cannot
contain its own name, so quote depth is a built-in level, and by
Proposition~\ref{prop:onelevel} communication raises it at most one level
at a time. The leak is the duplicator, which contracts \emph{without}
climbing. Since Meredith--Radestock is a recipe for generating a
namespace logic from a term language rather than a single logic
\cite{NamespaceLogic}, a light version is a choice of parameters:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{levels are quote depth}, which rho already supplies;
\item \emph{contraction must climb}: a communication that copies a
received name must place the copies one level up, so
$\out{x}{\drp{y}}$ in the duplicator does not type and a duplicating
process must wrap what it sends;
\item \emph{depth is bounded independently of the input}: $\enc{\Phi}$ is
typed at constant depth $k$.
\end{itemize}
In combinator terms, we expect the discipline to bound the number of
alternations of $\cdd$ and $\cev$. Doubling then fits at most $k$ rounds, and Asperti's
$\mathit{two}^n$ is exactly the input-dependent depth the discipline
forbids.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:light}
An experimenter typable in the light namespace logic at depth $k$ runs in
phlogiston polynomial in the size of its input, with degree depending on
$k$, under any cost model that charges communications.
\end{conjecture}

\noindent
Under Conjecture~\ref{conj:light}, together with the converse --- every
polynomial-phlogiston reflective experimenter is equivalent to one of
light type, as light affine logic is complete for polynomial time ---
Theorem~\ref{thm:whitebox} has a typed form: $\Pclass = \NPclass$ if and only if some reflective experimenter of
light type decides $\ABS$ on specimens over $\{\cm, \csy\}$ --- that is,
certifies non-derivability in one-shot Horn logic. The restatement is
still tautological, but the cost model has moved from the runtime into a
judgement that can be checked. Light affine logic also restricts boxes to
at most one free assumption; whether the rho analog --- a quoted process
sent at a level mentions at most one name from the level below --- is
natural, or too strong to express the specimens at all, is the first
thing to test.

\subsection{The barriers, located}\label{sec:barriers}

The correspondences in this subsection are analogies; we record them
because they say where in the apparatus each barrier would bite.
\emph{Relativisation} is interaction: arguments uniform over interaction
regimes treat the specimen as an oracle and can separate unconditionally
(Theorem~\ref{thm:blackbox}) while saying nothing about reflection
(Remark~\ref{rem:oracle}). \emph{Aggregation} is collapse: graded
readouts that identify resolutions are the apparatus in which an analog
of algebrisation would sit, and Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} shows
they must be costed by sharing, not by communication. \emph{Natural
proofs} would be experiments that certify hardness through a large,
constructive property of the table of outcomes over all knockouts
\cite{RazborovRudich97}; making that precise is open.

\section{Living in either world}\label{sec:worlds}
% =====================================================================

Three cautions frame what follows. First, $\Pclass$ versus $\NPclass$ is
a worst-case question, and a mortal scientist lives in a distribution over
ecologies \cite[\S``Distributions over ecologies'']{Scientist}; what it experiences is an average over
that distribution, which is the territory of Impagliazzo's five worlds
\cite{Impagliazzo95}. Second, polynomial is not affordable: the budget is
finite in both worlds, and what changes is how the price of a universal
negative scales with the size of the specimen. Third, only reflection is
affected. Interaction regimes pay exponentially in both worlds
(Theorem~\ref{thm:blackbox}). The difference between the worlds is
mainly a difference in what it is worth to \emph{hold} a specimen, and in
what it costs to find one.

\subsection{A mortal scientist in a world where \texorpdfstring{$\Pclass = \NPclass$}{P = NP}}

\paragraph{The asymmetry survives in logic and collapses in price.} The
scientist's evidence is still asymmetric: a universal is still never
confirmed by running. But a reflective scientist holding a bounded
specimen can certify its universal negatives for a price polynomial in
its size, as it already could confirm an existential. Popper's
asymmetry becomes a fact about interaction only.

\paragraph{Holding is everything.} Because only reflection gains,
acquiring a specimen's code --- not its stack, which is what a harvest
takes, but a quoted copy of its program --- becomes the decisive act.
The pressure recorded in \cite[Prop.~Scientists are preferred
prey]{Scientist} and in its discussion of theory theft sharpens:
scientists, whose hypotheses are now cheap to check once held, become
even more strongly preferred prey. The
destructive assay still forces induction --- one specimen, one
measurement --- but that one measurement now yields complete bounded
predictions for every member of the specimen's kind that shares its code.

\paragraph{Concealment fails on two fronts.} Concealment by depth fails
once a specimen is held, since structure is cheap to certify. Concealment
by unforgeability weakens too: deriving a name that satisfies a
perceivable predicate is a search problem with a checkable witness, so
capability gating loses its force, and implementations whose unforgeable
names rest on one-way functions lose them outright. Only the structural
freshness of Proposition~\ref{prop:wf} survives. Foraging becomes cheap,
the profitable band widens, and predation pressure rises.

\paragraph{Imperfection must come from elsewhere.} The companion game
note found that a solved science is a dead ecology: a monoculture of
perfect players draws every game and starves \cite{GameNote}. In a world
where $\Pclass = \NPclass$, every bounded question with short
certificates --- including every universal negative about a bounded
specimen --- is decided polynomially by whoever holds the code. Games of
polynomial length are $\mathbf{PSPACE}$-hard and stay out of reach unless
$\Pclass = \mathbf{PSPACE}$, but the imperfection that keeps an ecology
alive can no longer come from the hardness of what can be checked. It must come from budgets, and from
the fact that most of what a scientist meets is met across a cut, by
interaction, where the exponential remains. The ecology stays alive where
specimens cannot be held.

\subsection{A mortal scientist in a world where \texorpdfstring{$\Pclass \neq \NPclass$}{P != NP}}

\paragraph{Universal negatives have a price floor.} Even with the code in
hand, certifying that a bounded specimen cannot do something costs, in the
worst case, more than any polynomial. The scientist lives with $\bot$ and
with universals that are only ever refuted. Its theories stay
defeasible, and the budget remains its inductive bias with complexity
reinforcing it rather than replacing it.

\paragraph{Synchronisation is camouflage.} By Proposition~\ref{prop:nl}
and Theorem~\ref{thm:sy}, a specimen that does not synchronise is
transparent to a reflective scientist: what it cannot do is cheap to
know once it is held. A specimen that races and
joins can hide what it cannot do. In such a world coordination is a
defensive adaptation, and a scientist does well to specialise on niches
whose inhabitants synchronise little, where its universals are cheap. The
search for science is then, in part, the search for tractable fragments
--- the light, stratified environments of \S\ref{sec:light} --- in which
universal negatives can be afforded.

\paragraph{Science does not complete.} Intrinsic hardness supplies the
imperfection the game note found load-bearing \cite{GameNote}, so the
ecology does not run down to a solved monoculture. Concealment is a price
rather than a wall, but at scale the price is real; and whether
capability gating holds depends on more than $\Pclass \neq \NPclass$,
namely on whether one-way functions exist \cite{Impagliazzo95}.

\paragraph{Rational epistemics.} The scientist should invest in
existential hypotheses, which are cheap to confirm, and hold universals as
priced bets. Knockouts, controls and pooling cut costs by structure and by
constants; they do not escape the exponent. The value of reflection is
real but bounded: reading a specimen buys universals only for specimens
that live in tractable fragments.

\paragraph{The ecology is the adversary.} Finally, the worst-case
caveat may matter less than it seems. Theorem~\ref{thm:blackbox} is an
adversary argument, and an ecology supplies an adversary: selection favours
prey whose universal negatives are expensive to certify, since those are
the prey whose defences cannot be cheaply ruled out. If that pressure is
strong enough, the distribution a mortal scientist actually meets drifts
toward hard specimens, and in an ecology the worst case is not exceptional
but evolved. We state this as a conjecture about ecologies rather than
about complexity classes: \emph{predation selects for synchronisation}.
$\Pclass \neq \NPclass$ alone does not make hard specimens easy to come
by --- in Impagliazzo's Heuristica they are rare --- but where they can
be generated, that selection would produce an opaque world.

% =====================================================================
\section{Open problems}\label{sec:open}
% =====================================================================

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \textbf{Joins as the parameter.} Prove or refute
Conjecture~\ref{conj:fpt}; the obstacle is circular support through
shared forwarders.
\item \textbf{Reading by guards.} Write out the reconstruction of a held
specimen by adaptive structural guards used in Theorem~\ref{thm:whitebox}.
\item \textbf{The remaining joins.} Write out the payload-routing gadgets
showing $\coNP$-hardness over $\{\cm, \cbl\}$, $\{\cm, \cbr\}$ and
$\{\cm, \cfw, \cev\}$.
\item \textbf{Equivariant stamping.} Realise (E3) for general rho
processes by a namespace-relative evaluation in which every name the copy
synthesises is stamped.
\item \textbf{Drop-only extensionality.} Prove
Conjecture~\ref{conj:dropOnly}, making the interaction tier an admissible
observer class in the sense of \cite{BIHOL}.
\item \textbf{Grades.} Run-subset grades (stamping, prefix deletion)
preserve Theorem~\ref{thm:blackbox}; weakening grades (dropped bindings,
collapse) defeat it on particular families. Characterise the weakening
grades that help on hard specimens, if any, and price them by sharing.
\item \textbf{Models.} Relate the deletion model of
Construction~\ref{con:deletion} to the accumulator and attenuator models
of \cite{FuzzyRho}, and to the lift of fuzzy rho to ci\,GSLTs with
binding.
\item \textbf{Families of stamped communications.} Define them, and prove
or refute Conjecture~\ref{conj:upto}.
\item \textbf{A light namespace logic.} Instantiate the recipe of
\cite{NamespaceLogic} with stratified contraction, and prove
Conjecture~\ref{conj:light}; decide whether the one-assumption restriction
is needed.
\item \textbf{The ecology as adversary.} Test, in the ensemble of
\cite{Scientist}, whether predation selects for synchronising prey.
\item \textbf{Cost hypotheses.} Verify Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:sim}
to~\ref{hyp:refl} for cost-accounted rho as implemented, with particular
attention to content-addressed sharing.
\end{enumerate}
