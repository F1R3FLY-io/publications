Yes. This is a much stronger paper. Most of the objections from my first review have been addressed substantively rather than cosmetically, and several corrections are unusually explicit about what the first version got wrong. My recommendation has moved from **“major revision / reject-and-resubmit” to “minor-to-moderate revision, with one remaining foundational issue in the quantum story and one concrete paper/code mismatch.”**

The classical part is now in good shape. I would be comfortable defending its central claims. The quantum part is also much more disciplined, but the revision has exposed a different question: **once rewrite-induced coherence is correctly removed, what role do the complex weights themselves actually play?**

## Overall assessment

The revision successfully fixes the major first-round defects:

* the quantum basis is now the reachable **configuration** set, so dynamic weights no longer imply a silently switching generator; 
* rates and amplitudes are separated by (\lambda(z)=|z|^2); 
* the bogus (|z|\le1) restriction is gone; ([GitHub][1])
* the (m^2) “bosonic enhancement” is withdrawn and replaced by root-of-summed-rate normalization; 
* rewrite diamonds are no longer claimed to create interference; 
* the Gillespie degeneration theorem no longer needs the previous diagonality hypothesis; 
* SPiM is now explicitly the summation-free theorem it actually proves; ([GitHub][2])
* Booleanity is correctly recast as the price of an *internal* statement of admissibility, not a necessity for weighting itself; ([GitHub][2])
* locality has been restricted to the structural fragment and AC-tree distance replaced by an invariant notion; ([GitHub][2])
* the self-transition diagonal has been fixed; ([GitHub][2])
* the neural response is now correctly exponential-saturating, not logistic; 
* and the Hebbian claim is appropriately weakened. 

That is an impressive amount of actual repair. More importantly, the revised paper is intellectually healthier: it repeatedly says “the first version claimed X; X does not follow; here is the corrected statement.” That gives me substantially more confidence in the remaining claims.

There are, however, two issues I think still need action before calling this finished.

# 1. The biggest remaining theoretical problem: (E_h\subseteq E) is inconsistent with the quotient

This is the sharpest issue I found.

At the beginning of the paper, (E) is the set of equations generating the structural congruence (\equiv). Terms/configurations in the basis are taken modulo that congruence. 

Then Definition 31/your revised Hamiltonian construction says:

[
E_h\subseteq E
]

and for (e\in E_h) you take configurations related by (e), but which are nevertheless distinct basis states, and use those equations for coherent hopping:

[
H=\sum_{e\in E_h}h_e
\sum_{\mathfrak c\equiv_e\mathfrak c'}
(|\mathfrak c'\rangle\langle\mathfrak c|
+|\mathfrak c\rangle\langle\mathfrak c'|).
]

The prose says equations “left un-quotiented” contribute to (H). 

But if (E_h\subseteq E), and (\equiv) is generated by (E), then an (E_h)-equation is **not left un-quotiented**. Its endpoints have already been identified in the basis.

You cannot simultaneously have

[
e\in E,\qquad
\equiv = \operatorname{Cong}(E),
]

and have (e)'s endpoints remain distinct vectors of (\ell^2(\mathcal B/!\equiv)).

This looks easy to repair formally, but it matters because the Hamiltonian is now the *entire source of quantum coherence*.

I think the definition you actually want is something like

[
E = E_q \sqcup E_h
]

where:

* (E_q) generates the structural congruence used in forming the basis;
* (E_h) is a separate symmetric relation retained as coherent dynamics.

Then

[
\mathcal B
==========

\mathrm{Reach}(\mathfrak c_0)/{\equiv_{E_q}},
]

and (H) is generated from the residual (E_h)-edges between those quotient classes.

That would make your revised slogan precise:

> a weighted GSLT is quantum to the extent that its presentation retains symmetric relations as coherent dynamics rather than quotienting by them.

As currently written, “withholds equations from the quotient” is conceptually clear, but the formal declaration (E_h\subseteq E) contradicts it. 

I would consider this a **required correction**, not a stylistic suggestion.

# 2. There is now a deeper conceptual question: why are the weights complex at all?

The revised normalization correctly removes rewrite-induced interference. You now state:

> “no coherence whatever arises from the rewrite relation, and all of it is carried by the Hamiltonian.”



Good.

But notice the consequence.

Your jump operator has, for a class,

[
L_{r,\varphi}
=============

e^{i\theta}
\sum_{\mathfrak c,\mathfrak c''}
\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathfrak c\to\mathfrak c''}}
,|\mathfrak c''\rangle\langle\mathfrak c|.
]



If (e^{i\theta}) is an **overall phase on one Lindblad operator**, it is physically irrelevant:

[
(e^{i\theta}L)\rho(e^{i\theta}L)^\dagger
========================================

L\rho L^\dagger
]

and

[
(e^{i\theta}L)^\dagger(e^{i\theta}L)=L^\dagger L.
]

Your implementation explicitly acknowledges this: `with_phase` is described as a carrier check, and its documentation correctly says a global jump-operator phase cannot affect populations or create cancellation. ([GitHub][3])

So if all coherence comes from (H), and the phases attached to refinement weights are global phases of their respective Lindblad operators, **the complex phase of (w(\varphi)) has no observable effect whatsoever.**

That means the quantum construction, at present, can equivalently be parameterised by ordinary nonnegative rates:

[
\lambda_\varphi\in\mathbb R_{\ge0},
]

with exactly the same Lindbladian.

The genuinely quantum datum is (H), not (w\in\mathbb C).

This is not a correctness failure. It is a **conceptual identity crisis** for the phrase “complex-weighted GSLT.”

The paper currently says:

> “What is new is the phase that (\lambda) discards, and the linear structure in which a phase can act.”



But under your own subsequent construction, that phase does not act.

I would change the presentation in one of two ways.

The conservative option is to say openly:

> The quantum lift does not require complex rewrite weights. Its dissipative part is canonically determined by the classical rates; quantum structure enters through retained coherent equations encoded by (H). Complex amplitudes provide a compatible parametrisation of rates via (|z|^2), but class-global phases are gauge.

I actually think that makes the paper **stronger**, because it identifies exactly where the quantumness resides.

The ambitious alternative is to make phases source- or derivation-dependent so they can affect coherences. But that immediately reopens the issues you correctly closed in v2, and I would not recommend doing it in this paper. Your Open Thread on per-derivation amplitudes is the right place. 

So my recommendation is: **embrace the first interpretation**. The quantum construction is “classical dissipative rewriting + coherent equational dynamics,” not “complex weights make rewriting quantum.”

That is cleaner.

# 3. There is a real paper/code mismatch involving complex phases

This one is concrete.

The paper says a configuration contains its weight map, and in the quantum construction the Hilbert basis consists of full configurations. That is exactly the correct repair. 

The Rust `WeightMap` really can contain a `Complex(re, im)` value. ([GitHub][1])

But its configuration fingerprint is:

```rust
.map(|((r, i), v)| format!("{}:{}={:.6}", r.0, i, v.rate()))
```

In other words, it fingerprints only `v.rate()`. For a complex value that is

[
|z|^2.
]

([GitHub][1])

Therefore

[
z=1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
z=-1
]

or

[
z=1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
z=i
]

produce the **same configuration identity**.

That directly contradicts the paper's assertion that the basis is keyed by configurations including their complex weight maps.

It also means that if an update changes phase without changing modulus, the exhaustive explorer can collapse two states that the paper regards as distinct.

This is particularly striking because the quantum module comments say its basis is keyed by marking **and weight-map fingerprint** to distinguish plastic configurations. ([GitHub][3]) The fingerprint itself is currently lossy.

The fix is straightforward: fingerprint the actual `RateValue`, including the real/complex tag and both complex components, preferably using exact bit representations rather than `"{:.6}"`.

For example conceptually:

```text
R:<f64 bits>
C:<re bits>:<im bits>
```

rather than rate-only decimal formatting.

The six-decimal rounding is itself another possible state collision even for real maps:

[
1.0000001,;1.0000002
]

collapse under `"{:.6}"`.

If graph identity is semantic state identity, this needs to be collision-free for representable `RateValue`s, not display-friendly.

I would classify this as a **correctness bug in the implementation**.

# 4. The implementation currently discards weight-map phase when constructing (L)

The code builds jump operators from the **classical graph edge rates**:

```rust
.entry((e.to, e.from))
...
+= e.rate;

...
m.add_to(to, from, C::real(rate.sqrt()));
```

([GitHub][3])

So the jump matrices initially contain only positive-real (\sqrt{\lambda}). Complex phases from the `WeightMap` never make it into them.

`with_phase` can subsequently apply one manually selected phase to an entire refinement-class operator. ([GitHub][3])

That is not the same thing as implementing the paper's statement that a refinement's complex weight (z=|z|e^{i\theta}) determines the phase of the corresponding jump operator.

Now, because of the point above, this currently does **not alter the Lindbladian** if the phase is class-global. So the implementation's population dynamics and Lindbladian can still be correct despite dropping it.

But then the README's claim that the complex codomain is implemented is a little misleading. What is actually implemented is approximately:

[
\mathbb C
\xrightarrow{|z|^2}
\mathbb R_{\ge0}
\xrightarrow{\sqrt{\cdot}}
L,
]

plus an optional manually applied class-global phase.

The distinction should either be fixed or documented.

And again this reinforces my larger recommendation: declare class-global weight phases gauge, and you no longer need to pretend preserving them is semantically load-bearing.

# 5. The new “degeneracy proposition” should be softened

I agree with the **result**:

[
m\text{ equivalent redexes of rate }\lambda
\quad\leadsto\quad
m\lambda,
]

not (m^2\lambda).

And I agree that if your criterion is conservativity over the classical model, root-of-sum is the appropriate grouped-channel normalization. 

I'm less persuaded by the proof rhetoric:

> “Option (A) double-counts.”

and the invocation of second quantisation as though this uniquely establishes the semantics.

The stronger argument is the one you give immediately afterward:

> **we impose conservativity over the classical shadow.**

That is clean, operational, and testable. 

The Fock-space analogy

[
a|m\rangle=\sqrt m|m-1\rangle
]

is suggestive, but your configuration Hilbert space has not been constructed as a bosonic Fock space. You have a basis indexed by rewrite configurations. The (\sqrt m) scaling agrees with bosonic occupation-number normalization, but that analogy is not itself a derivation of your normalization.

I would therefore rename Proposition 38-ish from something like:

> “The degeneracy factor is a normalisation artefact”

to:

> **“Conservativity fixes the grouped-channel normalisation.”**

Then prove:

[
|\langle c'|L|c\rangle|^2
=========================

\sum_{\text{derivations }c\to c'}\lambda_d.
]

That is all you need.

The bosonic comparison can remain a remark.

# 6. The configuration-space fix is genuinely good, but trace state must be included everywhere once extended updates are used

The paper correctly observes in §8.8 that STDP requires enlarging configuration from

[
(P,w)
]

to

[
(P,w,S).
]



The Rust `Configuration` already contains a `TraceSummary`, which is good. ([GitHub][1])

I would just make the paper's quantum definition consistent with that generalized notion from the start.

Currently Definition 29 says configurations are term + weight map in the main development, while later the STDP extension enlarges them.

The most future-proof formulation is:

[
\mathfrak c=(P,\xi)
]

where (\xi) is all finite-dimensional deterministic auxiliary state required by the decoration—weight map at minimum, trace summaries when present.

Then the quantum construction uses reachable **extended configurations**.

Otherwise a reader can legitimately ask whether a quantum model using the extended update signature once again forgets state needed to keep the generator fixed.

Your implementation actually seems ahead of the paper on this point.

# 7. I like the new Boolean “dilemma,” but one sentence still says “weighting forces the choice”

The revised §3.3 is good. It explicitly says the first version's necessity claim did not follow, explains internal versus external admissibility, and identifies specification closure as the price of the latter. ([GitHub][2])

But the background still says:

> “§3.3 shows that weighting *forces* the choice.”



This is easy to misread as the old claim.

I'd use:

> “§3.3 describes the choice between internal Boolean admissibility and an external admissibility judgment.”

Much harder to misconstrue.

One further point: your external checker is explicitly a **sound refuter, not a proof**—it tests supplied witnesses and silence does not establish admissibility. The DESIGN file is admirably explicit about this. ([GitHub][4])

That means the implementation currently cannot actually guarantee Proposition 7's precondition for arbitrary user theories. I think the paper should foreground this a little more when claiming the implementation enforces the partition discipline. It can detect violations in its tested domain; it cannot establish semantic exhaustiveness/exclusivity in general.

# 8. The locality repair is much better, though the proof is still sketch-level

This was one of my stronger objections last time, and I now think the statement is basically defensible.

You:

* prohibit modalities and fixed points from keys;
* distinguish keys from properties;
* replace term-tree distance by an AC-compatible component distance;
* explicitly explain why the earlier lemma failed.

([GitHub][2])

That's a real fix.

What remains is proof detail. The claim that (\delta) is (\equiv)-invariant because its witnessing decompositions are exactly those quantified over by generated connectives is plausible but compressed. If this were headed for a formal-methods venue rather than a working note, I'd want the distance formally defined over equivalence classes/decomposition graphs and a proper induction theorem for satisfaction locality.

For the current paper, I would not block on that.

# 9. The self-transition treatment is now careful and interesting

The distinction between the generator eliminating fictitious self-jumps and the SSA retaining them is a good correction. ([GitHub][2])

The implementation documents the same convention: self loops remain in quantum jump channels and in sampling, while the classical generator diagonal is assembled from genuine off-diagonal exits. ([GitHub][3])

One wording issue: saying the two conventions “differ … in sojourn statistics” could confuse CTMC readers because the **state holding-time distribution** after marginalizing fictitious events is unchanged; what changes is the distribution of recorded event intervals if self-events count as events.

I'd say “recorded inter-event statistics” rather than “sojourn statistics.”

# 10. The neural section is now substantially better

This part improved a lot.

The paper now explicitly derives

[
1-e^{-\Delta x}
]

and calls it exponential saturation, with a remarkably clear correction of the previous logistic mistake. 

The Hebbian section now distinguishes local transmission-dependent potentiation from true pre/post firing correlation. 

And the quantum neural section now says the uncomfortable but correct thing:

> **as actually presented, the quantum network adds nothing because (H=0).**



That is excellent scientific hygiene.

In fact, I think this insight should affect the abstract slightly. Right now the abstract still gives “quantum execution” comparable billing with the stochastic network, while the actual worked network has no nontrivial quantum dynamics beyond its classical population shadow.

You could say that directly:

> “The worked neural model exercises the stochastic and adaptive machinery; its standard equational presentation has (H=0), so its quantum lift is deliberately a control case rather than an example of coherent neural dynamics.”

That prevents a reader from expecting a “quantum neural network” result that §8.10 correctly says you do not have.

# 11. The implementation is much more serious than the previous prototype

There is a lot I like here.

The design now has:

* actual redex enumeration and multiplicity;
* configuration-keyed exploration;
* explicit partition/checkability machinery;
* context factors rather than separate propensities;
* extended update signatures;
* exhaustive generator construction;
* quantum jump operators/Lindbladian/unravelling;
* analytic fixtures;
* negative controls;
* a non-diagonal conservativity check;
* and explicit scientific ledgers recording hypotheses that were refuted during development.

([GitHub][4])

The birth–death Poisson-vs-geometric discriminating test is particularly good. It tests the bug that matters rather than just checking that the simulator runs. ([GitHub][4])

I also appreciate that the repository does **not** claim integration that has not happened: it says the generated logic is handwritten behind a port and the real workspace matcher differential remains blocked. ([GitHub][4])

That is exactly the kind of implementation-status honesty I wanted in the first review.

The biggest implementation risks I see now are therefore not architecture-level. They are:

1. configuration identity losing phase and precision;
2. quantum model construction dropping map phases;
3. the claimed “generic over GSLTs” property remaining an architectural argument rather than a demonstrated generated-logic integration;
4. the real interpreter differential still unexecuted.

The DESIGN file itself already admits the latter two. ([GitHub][4])

# 12. One status claim in the README is too broad

The README says:

> “Everything the note specifies is implemented except the surface syntax…”

and only afterward qualifies that the generated logic is handwritten. ([GitHub][5])

Given the phase issue above and the unrun real matcher differential, I'd soften this to:

> “The simulator semantics described by the note are implemented, subject to the limitations below; surface syntax, OSLF-generated logic integration, and the workspace faithfulness differential remain open.”

That is closer to what the code actually demonstrates.

# My new referee verdict

If I separate the paper into components:

| Component                      | v1 assessment                      | v2 assessment                                                       |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Logical refinement / partition | promising, overclaimed             | **strong**                                                          |
| Propensity / Gillespie         | strong with issues                 | **strong**                                                          |
| Dynamic maps / Markov state    | strong                             | **strong**                                                          |
| SPiM relationship              | oversold                           | **correctly scoped**                                                |
| Locality / incremental story   | unsupported generality             | **plausible and appropriately restricted**                          |
| Neural example                 | interesting but rhetorically loose | **good worked example**                                             |
| Quantum state construction     | fundamentally broken               | **substantially repaired**                                          |
| Rewrite interference           | incorrect                          | **correctly withdrawn**                                             |
| Quantum normalization          | incorrect                          | **much better; justify via conservativity, not Fock analogy**       |
| Quantum coherence              | confused                           | **now located cleanly in (H), but (E_h/E) formalism needs repair**  |
| Implementation                 | prototype far behind paper         | **credible independent simulator with identifiable remaining gaps** |

So I no longer think this paper needs a conceptual rewrite.

I think it needs **one more focused revision**.

The two things I would insist on before considering it stable are:

1. **formalize the split between quotiented equations and Hamiltonian equations**—something like (E_q) versus (E_h); and
2. **decide what you really mean by complex weights.**

My preference on (2) is not to invent new quantum machinery. Say plainly that after imposing conservativity, the phases of refinement-class weights are gauge and the dissipative semantics factors through

[
\mathbb C\xrightarrow{|,\cdot,|^2}\mathbb R_{\ge0}.
]

Then the true quantum extension is

[
\boxed{
\text{classical weighted rewrite dissipator}
+
\text{coherent residual equational dynamics}
}
]

That is, to my eye, a **much cleaner and more interesting result** than “replace rates by complex amplitudes and get quantum rewriting.”

It also aligns with what the implementation actually does: its jump construction starts from aggregated classical edge rates, while (H) is supplied separately. ([GitHub][3])

Fix the configuration fingerprint so it represents full semantic state, adjust the few corresponding claims, and I would be down to ordinary referee-level objections rather than foundational ones.

One final reaction: the revision note and the scientific ledger materially improve the work. They make it possible to see not only what changed but **why particular seductive claims were rejected by the mathematics or implementation**. ([GitHub][4]) I would keep that character even if you eventually polish some of the explicit self-correction language for publication.
